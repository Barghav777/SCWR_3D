\title{\textbf{Case-disjoint benchmark of surrogate models for flow and thermal fields in supercritical $\mathrm{CO}_2$ natural-circulation loops}}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Surrogate models for supercritical carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{sCO}_2$) natural-circulation loops (NCLs) can replace day-long steady-state conjugate computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations with fast post-training field evaluations, yet most published evaluations rely on random node-level train--test splits or single pooled metrics that mask generalization failure across unseen operating conditions. Here we present a case-disjoint surrogate benchmark built from 42 legacy steady-state RANS simulations solved with a $k$--$\varepsilon$ family turbulence model ($k$ and $\varepsilon$ are exported) and a Span--Wagner equation of state verified for internal thermodynamic consistency against \texttt{CoolProp} (median $0.0008\%$) across three heater--cooler configurations (\texttt{HHHC}, \texttt{HHVC}, and \texttt{VHVC}) and two loop scales. Across three random seeds, we benchmark 19 surrogate model variants (16 cross-configuration pooled models, including multiplicative \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} and its trunk-matched input-concatenation ablation \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP}, plus 3 within-group mesh-aligned models: \texttt{POD-RBF}, \texttt{POD-GPR}, and \texttt{Nearest case}) alongside a specified $64\times 64\times 16$ voxel round-trip reconstruction reference (\texttt{Lattice floor (oracle)}) under five-fold stratified case-disjoint cross-validation and leave-one-group-out structural/regime shift. We show that random node splitting understates five-fold case-disjoint error across the three strictly matched-configuration tree baselines (\texttt{Random Forest}, \texttt{Extra Trees}, \texttt{CatBoost}) by $6.78\times\text{--}8.25\times$ on density, $11.29\times\text{--}30.08\times$ on gauge pressure, and $5.62\times\text{--}10.24\times$ on temperature in case-macro MAE ($6.78\times\text{--}10.37\times$ and $11.29\times\text{--}87.54\times$ when including \texttt{XGBoost}) because high-capacity models memorize case-specific operating levels from adjacent mesh nodes. We also show that separating closed-loop gauge pressure into its case-level thermal-pressurization offset and centered spatial residual---benchmarked against a zero-residual baseline---prevents small within-case hydrostatic variations from distorting model rankings. Across the eight exported CFD fields, no single architecture wins universally: among cross-configuration pooled models, \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP} achieves the lowest case-macro MAE on density ($26.3\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) and temperature ($5.14^\circ\text{C}$) whereas \texttt{DeepONet} ($0.837$) and \texttt{PINN continuity} ($1.350$) achieve the lowest median case nMAE on those fields; \texttt{Extra Trees} best predicts case-level gauge pressure ($83.2\text{ kPa}$ case-macro MAE); and multiplicative feature-modulated coordinates (\texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP}) achieve the lowest case-macro MAE and median case nMAE among pooled models across both turbulence fields ($k, \varepsilon$) and all three velocity components ($u, v, w$), while within-group mesh-aligned \texttt{POD-RBF} and \texttt{POD-GPR} achieve the lowest median case nMAE on temperature ($0.974$), $\varepsilon$ ($0.150$ for \texttt{POD-GPR}; $0.177$ for \texttt{POD-RBF}), and $k$ ($0.237$ for \texttt{POD-RBF}; $0.245$ for \texttt{POD-GPR}) when interpolating on a fixed loop mesh.
\end{abstract}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} supercritical $\mathrm{CO}_2$, natural-circulation loop, CFD surrogate benchmark, case-disjoint validation, neural operators, proper orthogonal decomposition

\section{Introduction}
Natural-circulation loops (NCLs) operating with supercritical fluids remove heat without mechanical pumps by balancing buoyancy forces between heated and cooled sections against frictional and local losses~\cite{swapnalee2012generalized,vijayan2002experimental,pioro2007heat}. Supercritical carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{sCO}_2$, critical point $T_c = 304.13\text{ K} = 30.98^\circ\text{C}$, $p_c = 7.3773\text{ MPa} = 73.773\text{ bar}$) is widely used as a scaled simulant fluid for supercritical water reactor (SCWR) thermal-hydraulics and compact power cycles because its lower critical point permits experiments and conjugate CFD studies near the pseudocritical locus~\cite{span1996new,dostal2004supercritical,bae2005mixed}. Near the pseudocritical temperature, steep variations in density $\rho$, specific heat $c_p$, viscosity $\mu$, and thermal conductivity $k$ strongly couple buoyancy gradients with turbulence production and heat transfer~\cite{kurganov2013heat,sarkar2014entropy,chen2013numerical}.

Because resolving conjugate heat transfer between the primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ pipe, the solid metallic wall, and the secondary cooling-water jacket requires fine near-wall meshes and real-gas property evaluations, each steady-state RANS simulation in our dataset requires roughly 24 hours of compute time. Data-driven spatial surrogates---including gradient-boosted trees~\cite{friedman2001greedy,chen2016xgboost,prokhorenkova2018catboost}, coordinate neural networks~\cite{raissi2019physics,tancik2020fourier,perez2018film}, neural operators~\cite{lu2021learning,li2021fourier,wen2022ufno,wu2024transolver}, and proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) reduced-order models~\cite{berkooz1993proper,audouze2009nonintrusive}---provide fast post-training field evaluations once fit. However, three practical issues complicate surrogate benchmarking on closed natural-circulation loops: (i) random node-level train--test splits leak case-specific operating levels across neighboring mesh nodes; (ii) pooled metrics such as $R^2$ conflate between-case operating-level shifts with within-case spatial profile accuracy, while naive within-case normalization breaks down for gauge pressure in closed loops; and (iii) slender circular pipes ($D_i/L \sim 10^{-2}$) and varying heater--cooler orientations create different inductive-bias requirements for thermodynamic state fields versus velocity and turbulence fields.

To address these issues, this paper contributes: (1) a controlled comparison of 80/20 random-node splitting against five-fold case-disjoint cross-validation across three strictly matched-configuration tree ensembles, \texttt{XGBoost}, and two linear/constant controls; (2) a physical characterization and two-part evaluation protocol for closed-loop gauge pressure referenced against a zero-residual baseline; and (3) a three-seed benchmark of 19 surrogate model variants (16 cross-configuration pooled models and 3 within-group mesh-aligned models, plus a specified $64\times 64\times 16$ voxel round-trip reconstruction reference) across eight CFD fields under both five-fold case-disjoint interpolation and leave-one-group-out structural/regime shift.

\section{Physical Domain and $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ Natural-Circulation Loop Dataset}
\subsection{Loop Geometry, CFD Solver Configuration, and Primary Fluid Domain}
The dataset consists of 42 legacy converged steady-state RANS conjugate heat-transfer simulations of closed rectangular $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ natural-circulation loops solved in ANSYS Fluent and exported on cross-sectional planes via ANSYS CFD-Post. All simulations use a $k$--$\varepsilon$ family turbulence model ($k$ and $\varepsilon$ are exported as RANS closure variables rather than DNS/LES or experimental measurements) and a Span--Wagner equation of state verified for internal thermodynamic consistency against \texttt{CoolProp}~\cite{span1996new,bell2014pure} (median relative density discrepancy $0.0008\%$ between exported $(T, p_{\mathrm{abs}})$ and $\rho$) for the primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ fluid. Because the dataset is curated from archived CFD-Post plane exports, this \texttt{CoolProp} audit verifies equation-of-state consistency of the exported fields rather than mesh-independence or experimental flow validation.

The 42 cases cover three heater--cooler configurations---horizontal heater/horizontal cooler (\texttt{HHHC}), horizontal heater/vertical cooler (\texttt{HHVC}), and vertical heater/vertical cooler (\texttt{VHVC})---across two loop scales (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}): 29 small-loop cases ($L_x \times L_y = 0.8936 \times 1.0000\text{ m}$, $D_i = 15.00\text{ mm}$) and 13 large-loop \texttt{VHVC} cases ($L_x \times L_y = 3.0100 \times 4.0800\text{ m}$, $D_i = 13.84\text{ mm}$). Complete radial dimensions of the stainless-steel wall and cooling-water jacket, axial heater/cooler spans, and sampling-plane counts (97 planes in small loops; 142 planes in the large loop) are given in Supplementary Section~S1.

Each simulation is indexed by four boundary parameters encoded in its case name \texttt{\{CONFIG\}\_P\{Q\}\_\{p\}\_bar\_\{mdot\}kgs\_T\_\{Tin\}}: heater power $Q \in [10, 2500]\text{ W}$, nominal operating pressure $p_{\mathrm{op}} \in \{80, 85, 90, 100\}\text{ bar}$, cooling-water mass flow rate $\dot{m} \in [0.010, 0.150]\text{ kg s}^{-1}$, and cooling-water inlet temperature $T_{\mathrm{in}} \in \{283.5, 300, 305, 315\}\text{ K}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pairplot}). At each node $(x,y,z)$, eight CFD target fields are exported: density $\rho\text{ (kg m}^{-3}\text{)}$, gauge pressure $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}\text{ (Pa)}$, temperature $T\text{ (}^\circ\text{C)}$, turbulence eddy dissipation $\varepsilon\text{ (m}^2\text{ s}^{-3}\text{)}$, turbulence kinetic energy $k\text{ (m}^2\text{ s}^{-2}\text{)}$, and velocity components $(u,v,w)\text{ (m s}^{-1}\text{)}$. Removing solid wall nodes (\texttt{null} velocity/turbulence, $\rho \approx 7970\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) and outer cooling-water jacket nodes ($\rho > 950\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) and deduplicating intersecting plane coordinates yields $5,451,341$ unique primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ nodes ($\rho \in [153.2, 892.5]\text{ kg m}^{-3}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:distributions}).

\subsection{Behavior of Exported Gauge Pressure Across Cases}
To separate directly verified properties of the exported CFD files from inferred solver-configuration explanations, we first report the quantities computed directly from the 42 plane-export datasets. In all 42 simulations, the local absolute pressure matching the exported $\mathrm{CO}_2$ density is the sum of the nominal operating pressure $p_{\mathrm{op}}$ and the exported gauge pressure $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mathbf{x})$:
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathbf{x}) = p_{\mathrm{op}} + p_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mathbf{x}).
  \label{eq:piezometric}
\end{equation}
Evaluating $(T(\mathbf{x}), p_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathbf{x}))$ in \texttt{CoolProp}~\cite{span1996new,bell2014pure} reproduces the exported CFD density $\rho(\mathbf{x})$ across all 42 cases to a median relative difference of $0.0008\%$ ($0.00004\%\text{--}0.0015\%$). Directly computing the within-case population standard deviation $\sigma_c(p_{\mathrm{gauge}})$ and case mean $\bar{p}_c$ shows a stark scale separation: within any single case $c$, $\sigma_c(p_{\mathrm{gauge}})$ is only $0.15\text{--}0.39\text{ kPa}$ in the 29 small loops and $1.45\text{--}8.79\text{ kPa}$ in the 13 large loops (42-case arithmetic mean $\bar{\sigma}_c = 1.51\text{ kPa}$; root-mean-square $2.89\text{ kPa}$), whereas across cases $\bar{p}_c$ spans $1.42\text{ kPa}$ to $1.763\text{ MPa}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}). Physically, because a full hydrostatic head $\rho g H$ over $H = 1\text{--}4\text{ m}$ would produce $7\text{--}30\text{ kPa}$ of vertical variation, we infer that the Fluent setup subtracted a spatially varying hydrostatic reference field $\rho_0 \mathbf{g}\cdot\mathbf{x}$ (whereas subtracting any spatially constant offset would not alter $\sigma_c$):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Large loop vs.\ small loops (observed $\bar{p}_c$ ranges and inferred reference convention):} Directly from the exports, the 13 large-loop \texttt{VHVC} cases have $\bar{p}_c \in [1.42, 8.77]\text{ kPa}$ with $p_{\mathrm{gauge}} \approx 0\text{ Pa}$ near the top of the loop ($y = 4.08\text{ m}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}a), whereas in the 29 small-loop cases (\texttt{HHHC}, \texttt{HHVC}, and small \texttt{VHVC}) $\bar{p}_c$ spans $6.1\text{ kPa}$ to $1.763\text{ MPa}$ and rises strongly with the loop-mean temperature $\bar{T}_c$. We infer that the large-loop simulations applied a zero-gauge reference pressure pin near the top boundary, whereas the small-loop simulations evolved without a fixed zero-gauge pin so that $\bar{p}_c$ reflects thermal pressurization relative to the initial state.
  \item \textbf{Observed pressure--temperature slope vs.\ \texttt{CoolProp} constant-density derivative:} Directly fitting $\bar{p}_c$ versus $\bar{T}_c$ across the 17 small-loop cases at $p_{\mathrm{op}} = 90\text{ bar}$ and $T_{\mathrm{in}} = 305\text{ K}$ gives an \emph{empirical slope} of $0.214\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ ($0.235\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ across the five \texttt{HHVC} cases; Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}b). By comparison, the theoretical isochoric derivative $(\partial p/\partial T)_\rho$ evaluated in \texttt{CoolProp} at each case's exported plane-weighted mean state $(\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}, \bar{T}_c)$ is $0.315\text{--}0.365\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ (median $0.338\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$; $0.133\text{--}0.417\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ across all 29 small-loop cases in Supplementary Table~S0b). Directly inspecting the plane-weighted mean $\mathrm{CO}_2$ density $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ (note that $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ is the arithmetic mean over exported CFD-Post sampling-plane nodes rather than a volume-integrated total mass inventory) shows that $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ varies across cases at the same $(p_{\mathrm{op}}, T_{\mathrm{in}})$: at $90\text{ bar}, 305\text{ K}$ it spans $671.6\text{--}704.0\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ ($\mathrm{SD}=9.79\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) in \texttt{HHHC}, $681.2\text{--}717.8\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ ($\mathrm{SD}=16.66\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) in \texttt{HHVC}, and $696.8\text{--}712.1\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ ($\mathrm{SD}=6.62\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) in small \texttt{VHVC} (and $161.5\text{--}860.8\text{ kg m}^{-3}$, $\mathrm{SD}=270.83\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ in \texttt{VHVC/large}). Because warmer small-loop cases exhibit slightly lower $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ ($\mathrm{d}\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane}}/\mathrm{d}\bar{T} < 0$), first-order expansion $\mathrm{d}\bar{p} \approx (\partial p/\partial T)_\rho\,\mathrm{d}\bar{T} + (\partial p/\partial \rho)_T\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\rho}$ accounts for why the observed across-case slope ($0.214\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$) is lower than the pure constant-density derivative ($0.315\text{--}0.365\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$).
  \item \textbf{Near-stagnant steady solutions in \texttt{HHVC} at $20\text{ W}$ and $30\text{ W}$:} Directly from the exported velocity and temperature fields in the five \texttt{HHVC} cases at $90\text{ bar}$ and $305\text{ K}$, the $Q = 20\text{ W}$ and $30\text{ W}$ runs (\texttt{HHVC\_P20\_90\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_305} and \texttt{HHVC\_P30\_90\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_305}) are near-stagnant steady solutions (mean speed $\overline{|\mathbf{u}|} = 0.9\text{--}1.1\text{ mm s}^{-1}$, $\bar{T}_c = 31.92\text{--}31.94^\circ\text{C}$, $\bar{p}_c = 6.1\text{--}9.2\text{ kPa}$), whereas the $10\text{ W}$, $40\text{ W}$, and $50\text{ W}$ runs have sustained buoyant circulation ($\overline{|\mathbf{u}|} = 0.044\text{--}0.107\text{ m s}^{-1}$, $\bar{T}_c = 33.58\text{--}39.36^\circ\text{C}$, $\bar{p}_c = 0.361\text{--}1.763\text{ MPa}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}b).
\end{itemize}

\section{Benchmark Methodology, Models, and Evaluation Protocol}
\subsection{Validation Protocols, Leakage Prevention, and Evaluation Metrics}
Every model predicts the eight target fields $\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{c}) \in \mathbb{R}^8$ at coordinates $\mathbf{x} = (x,y,z)$ given the case descriptor $\mathbf{c} = (Q, p_{\mathrm{op}}, \dot{m}, T_{\mathrm{in}}, L_x, L_y, D_i, \text{one-hot config})$. We evaluate three validation protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Five-fold stratified case-disjoint cross-validation:} For each seed $s \in \{2026, 2027, 2028\}$, the 42 cases are partitioned into five folds ($9, 9, 8, 8, 8$ test cases) stratified by the four groups (\texttt{HHHC/small}, \texttt{HHVC/small}, \texttt{VHVC/small}, \texttt{VHVC/large}). Each seed independently re-randomizes the five-fold case partition, the four validation cases per fold, the training node subsample ($30,000$ nodes/case in GPU 3-seed CV; $20,000$ in the single-seed CPU split test), and model initialization. Held-out test cases are evaluated on 100\% of their mesh nodes ($5,451,341$ out-of-fold predictions per seed).
  \item \textbf{Within-group mesh-aligned cross-validation:} Mesh-aligned models (\texttt{POD-RBF}, \texttt{POD-GPR}, \texttt{Nearest case}) require a shared mesh ordering and therefore train and predict strictly within the matching configuration/scale group of each five-fold split.
  \item \textbf{Leave-one-group-out structural/regime shift:} All cases in one structural group (\texttt{HHHC/small}, $n=20$; \texttt{HHVC/small}, $n=5$; \texttt{VHVC/small}, $n=4$; or \texttt{VHVC/large}, $n=13$) are held out while the 16 cross-configuration pooled models train on the remaining three groups ($16\text{ models} \times 8\text{ fields} = 128$ model--field evaluations per group). Because each group pairs a distinct heater--cooler arrangement or loop scale with its own operating-parameter range (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}), this protocol measures combined structural and operating-regime shift rather than pure geometric extrapolation at fixed operating conditions.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Verified train-only preprocessing and split hygiene:} In the benchmark pipeline (\texttt{kaggle\_clean\_case\_cv\_predictions.ipynb}), four programmatic safeguards prevent data leakage across splits: (i) explicit set-disjointness assertions verify that training, validation, and test case ID sets are pairwise disjoint on every fold and group holdout before any preprocessing (\texttt{results/split\_assignments.csv}); (ii) validation-case selection (\texttt{pick\_val}) draws the four validation cases per fold exclusively from strata with at least eight non-test cases (\texttt{HHHC/small} and \texttt{VHVC/large}), ensuring that the small groups (\texttt{HHVC/small}, $n=5$; \texttt{VHVC/small}, $n=4$) retain all non-test cases ($4$ and $3\text{--}4$ training cases per fold) for model fitting; (iii) input feature standardization (\texttt{cf\_mean}, \texttt{cf\_scale}, with a zero-variance fallback \texttt{cf\_scale = 1.0}) and target scaling (\texttt{TargetScaler}: $\operatorname{arcsinh}(y/\operatorname{median}(|y_{\mathrm{train}}|))$ followed by z-score standardization on heavy-tailed $\varepsilon$ and $k$, and z-score standardization on the other six fields, inverted via \texttt{np.clip(z, -12.0, 12.0)} during prediction) are fit strictly on the fold's training cases (\texttt{split["train\_ids"]}); and (iv) for within-group mesh-aligned models (\texttt{run\_group\_method}), operating-parameter standardization, snapshot mean subtraction, truncated SVD basis construction ($\le 12$ modes, $99.99\%$ energy), and leave-one-out RBF hyperparameter selection (\texttt{tune\_rbf}) are computed exclusively on the training cases belonging to that group ($\{c \in \mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{train}} : g(c) = g\}$).

For each held-out case $c$ ($N_c$ nodes, truth $y_{c,i}$, prediction $\hat{y}_{c,i}$, case mean $\bar{y}_c = N_c^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c} y_{c,i}$, and population within-case standard deviation $\sigma_c = \sqrt{N_c^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c}(y_{c,i}-\bar{y}_c)^2}$ with \texttt{ddof=0}), we compute case-level MAE, RMSE, case-normalized MAE ($\mathrm{nMAE}_c = \mathrm{MAE}_c / \sigma_c$), and node-pooled $R^2_{\mathrm{pooled}}$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MAE}_c = \frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c} |\hat{y}_{c,i} - y_{c,i}|, \quad
  \mathrm{RMSE}_c = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c} (\hat{y}_{c,i} - y_{c,i})^2}, \quad
  R^2_{\mathrm{pooled}} = 1 - \frac{\sum_{c,i}(\hat{y}_{c,i} - y_{c,i})^2}{\sum_{c,i}(y_{c,i} - \bar{y}_{\mathrm{all}})^2}.
\end{equation}
For each seed $s$, we aggregate across the 42 cases via the 42-case arithmetic mean for Macro MAE and Macro RMSE, the 42-case median and 90th percentile (P90) for $\mathrm{nMAE}_c$, and pooled $R^2_{\mathrm{pooled}}$ across all $5,451,341$ nodes, and then report the mean $\pm$ SD across the three seeds (in Supplementary Tables~S1--S7, 95\% case-bootstrap confidence intervals are formed by drawing $B=400$ resamples of the 42 held-out cases with replacement per seed and averaging the 2.5th and 97.5th percentile endpoints across the three seeds). Because $\bar{\sigma}_c(p_{\mathrm{gauge}}) = 1.51\text{ kPa}$ is small relative to between-case pressure shifts (Section~2.2), we suppress raw $\mathrm{nMAE}_c$ for $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}$ and decompose each case's pressure error into its case-mean level error $e_{\mathrm{level},c}$ and centered spatial residual $\tilde{p}_{c,i} = p_{c,i} - \bar{p}_c$ (satisfying $\mathrm{RMSE}_c^2 = (\hat{\bar{p}}_c - \bar{p}_c)^2 + \mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c}^2$ identically under \texttt{ddof=0}):
\begin{equation}
  e_{\mathrm{level},c} = |\hat{\bar{p}}_c - \bar{p}_c|, \quad
  \mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c}\bigl(\tilde{\hat{p}}_{c,i} - \tilde{p}_{c,i}\bigr)^2}, \quad
  \mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c} = \frac{\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c}}{\max(\sigma_c, 1\text{ kPa})}.
\end{equation}
For each seed, $e_{\mathrm{level},c}$ is averaged across the 42 cases, $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c}$ is summarized by its 42-case median, and physical-unit residual error is summarized by both the 42-case root-mean-square $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res,RMS}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{42}\sum_{c=1}^{42}\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c}^2}$ and the 42-case arithmetic mean $\overline{\mathrm{RMSE}}_{\mathrm{res}} = \frac{1}{42}\sum_{c=1}^{42}\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c}$ before averaging across the three seeds (both aggregations are reported in Supplementary Table~S8). Predicting a spatially constant pressure within each case ($\tilde{\hat{p}}_{c,i} = 0$, achieved by \texttt{Mean baseline}, where $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c} = \sigma_c$) defines the \textbf{zero-residual baseline}, which yields a three-seed mean $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res,RMS}}$ of $2.89\text{ kPa}$ ($2886\text{ Pa}$), a 42-case arithmetic-mean $\overline{\mathrm{RMSE}}_{\mathrm{res}}$ of $1.51\text{ kPa}$ ($1513\text{ Pa}$), and a median $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}}$ of $0.202$.

\subsection{Benchmark Model Suite, Optimization Protocol, and Verified Hyperparameters}
Table~\ref{tab:models} summarizes all 19 evaluated surrogate model variants (16 cross-configuration pooled models and 3 within-group aligned models) plus the non-competing \texttt{Lattice floor (oracle)} voxel round-trip reconstruction reference, together with parameter counts, per-fold execution times, and verified hyperparameters from the GPU and CPU benchmark scripts. All pointwise neural networks train with case-balanced mini-batches of $8,192$ nodes per step (drawing an equal number of nodes from each training case in the fold), cosine learning-rate decay, gradient clipping at $1.0$, and early stopping (patience 10, evaluated every 200 steps on the four held-out validation cases per fold, with the inner gradient-descent training loop capped at 2600 steps or $480\text{ s}$ [$8\text{ min}$]/fold on an NVIDIA P100 GPU; the total per-fold wall-clock times reported in Table~\ref{tab:models}, such as $666\text{ s}$ for \texttt{Latent Neural Operator} and $746\text{ s}$ for \texttt{GAMS-GNN-style}, additionally include multi-scale kNN/voxel graph construction, validation-set evaluations, and full-mesh out-of-fold inference across $1.1\text{--}1.8\text{ million}$ test nodes per fold). To compare multiplicative feature modulation against input concatenation at matched backbone width ($256$) and depth ($5$ residual blocks), \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} ($1,069,064$ parameters) modulates each hidden block by $\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)} \odot (1 + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{c})) + \boldsymbol{\beta}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{c})$~\cite{perez2018film}, whereas its trunk-matched ablation \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP} ($344,072$ parameters; $3.11\times$ fewer parameters because it omits the five per-block linear FiLM generators) concatenates $\mathbf{c}$ at the input layer. In \texttt{PINN continuity}, the steady mass-conservation residual is computed in physical units via automatic differentiation with respect to normalized coordinates $\tilde{x}_a \in [0,1]$ divided by each case's physical bounding-box span $\Delta x_{c,a}$: $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{cont}} = S_{\mathrm{flux}}^{-1}\sum_{a\in\{x,y,z\}} (\Delta x_{c,a})^{-1}\partial(\hat{\rho}\hat{u}_a)/\partial \tilde{x}_a$, where $S_{\mathrm{flux}} = (|\mu_\rho|+\sigma_\rho)\bar{\sigma}_u / \overline{\Delta x}$ normalizes the divergence residual before multiplying by $\lambda_{\mathrm{cont}} = 0.05$.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\subsection{Optimism of Node-Random Splits Versus Case-Disjoint Validation}
Table~\ref{tab:leakage} and Figure~\ref{fig:leakage} compare 80/20 random node splitting against five-fold case-disjoint cross-validation on the $5,451,341$ primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ nodes from the paired single-seed (\texttt{2026}) split-comparison benchmark (\texttt{results/co2\_candidate\_case\_and\_random\_node\_metrics.csv}). Whereas the two low-capacity controls (\texttt{Mean baseline} and \texttt{Ridge}) show error ratios near $1.0\times$ ($0.95\times\text{--}1.26\times$) because they cannot memorize local mesh neighborhoods, the three strictly matched-configuration tree models (\texttt{Random Forest}, \texttt{Extra Trees}, \texttt{CatBoost}, which use identical tree counts and depths in both protocols) suffer large error inflation under case-disjoint evaluation on thermodynamic state fields: $6.78\times\text{--}8.25\times$ on density, $11.29\times\text{--}30.08\times$ on gauge pressure, and $5.62\times\text{--}10.24\times$ on temperature in case-macro MAE ($3.52\times\text{--}4.05\times$ and $2.95\times\text{--}4.39\times$ in median case nMAE on $\rho$ and $T$). Including \texttt{XGBoost} (which used \texttt{max\_depth=6} on random-node splits vs.\ \texttt{max\_depth=8} on case-disjoint splits) widens the four-tree case-macro MAE inflation range to $6.78\times\text{--}10.37\times$ on $\rho$ and $11.29\times\text{--}87.54\times$ on $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}$. This inflation occurs because random node splits allow high-capacity trees to interpolate case-specific operating levels from adjacent mesh nodes of the same simulation.

Four main findings emerge from Table~\ref{tab:main} and Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Metric-specific winners on state fields ($\rho, T$) vs.\ kinematic/turbulence fields ($u, v, w, k, \varepsilon$):} On density and temperature, case-macro MAE and median case nMAE favor different pooled models because small within-case standard deviations in the small loops ($\sigma_c(T) \approx 0.25\text{--}2.5^\circ\text{C}$) penalize even modest operating-level bias: \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP} achieves the lowest pooled case-macro MAE on both $\rho$ ($26.3\text{ kg m}^{-3}$, $79.7\%$ reduction, P90 nMAE $3.74$, $R^2_{\mathrm{pooled}}=0.936$) and $T$ ($5.14^\circ\text{C}$, $65.5\%$ reduction, ahead of \texttt{FiLM-MLP} at $5.34^\circ\text{C}$ and \texttt{PINN continuity} at $5.48^\circ\text{C}$), whereas \texttt{DeepONet} achieves the lowest pooled median case nMAE on $\rho$ ($0.837 \pm 0.160$) and \texttt{PINN continuity} achieves the lowest pooled median case nMAE on $T$ ($1.350 \pm 0.044$; improved to $0.974 \pm 0.041$ by within-group \texttt{POD-RBF}). Across structural groups, \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} achieves low median nMAE on \texttt{VHVC/large} ($0.471$ on $\rho$, $0.482$ on $T$, $0.082$ on $u$, $0.088$ on $v$) and \texttt{VHVC/small} ($0.251$ on $\rho$, $0.500$ on $T$), whereas \texttt{HHHC/small} ($\rho$: $1.391$, $T$: $2.458$) and \texttt{HHVC/small} ($\rho$: $2.273$, $T$: $5.363$, containing the two near-stagnant $20\text{--}30\text{ W}$ cases) drive the P90 tail. On velocity and turbulence fields, \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} achieves both the lowest case-macro MAE and lowest median case nMAE among all 16 pooled models on $u$ ($0.0381\text{ m s}^{-1}$, nMAE $0.162$), $v$ ($0.0383\text{ m s}^{-1}$, nMAE $0.175$), $w$ ($1.43\times 10^{-3}\text{ m s}^{-1}$, nMAE $0.272$), $\varepsilon$ ($0.0978\text{ m}^2\text{ s}^{-3}$, nMAE $0.192$), and $k$ ($0.947\times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2\text{ s}^{-2}$, nMAE $0.242$). When interpolating within a fixed group mesh, \texttt{POD-GPR} achieves the lowest overall median case nMAE on $\varepsilon$ ($0.150 \pm 0.011$, case-macro MAE $0.0995\text{ m}^2\text{ s}^{-3}$, $R^2_{\mathrm{pooled}}=0.438$) and \texttt{POD-RBF} achieves the lowest median case nMAE on $k$ ($0.237 \pm 0.015$, followed by \texttt{FiLM-MLP} at $0.242$ and \texttt{POD-GPR} at $0.245$), although \texttt{POD-RBF} exhibits negative $R^2_{\mathrm{pooled}}$ on $\varepsilon$ ($-0.575$) and $k$ ($-0.070$) because unregularized Gaussian RBF extrapolation on boundary cases creates localized turbulence spikes (P90 nMAE $1.46$ and $1.25$) that inflate node-pooled squared error.
  \item \textbf{FiLM modulation vs.\ input concatenation at matched trunk depth/width:} Comparing \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} ($1,069,064$ params) against its trunk-matched input-concatenation ablation \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP} ($344,072$ params) shows that adding per-block affine FiLM generators ($3.11\times$ total parameter count at identical 5-block, width-256 backbone capacity) reduces median case nMAE on six of the seven spatially varying fields---$T$ ($1.467$ vs.\ $1.758$), $\varepsilon$ ($0.192$ vs.\ $0.226$), $k$ ($0.242$ vs.\ $0.273$), $u$ ($0.162$ vs.\ $0.224$), $v$ ($0.175$ vs.\ $0.230$), and $w$ ($0.272$ vs.\ $0.318$)---and lowers case-macro MAE on $\varepsilon, k, u, v, w$, whereas lower-parameter input concatenation yields lower case-macro MAE on $\rho$ ($26.3$ vs.\ $31.3\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) and $T$ ($5.14$ vs.\ $5.34^\circ\text{C}$). Because parameter count is not held constant between the two variants, these gains reflect the combined effect of multiplicative conditioning and the additional FiLM generator parameters.
  \item \textbf{Gauge-pressure level vs.\ spatial residual and the zero-residual baseline:} Total gauge-pressure case-macro MAE ($83.2\text{ kPa}$ for \texttt{Extra Trees}, a $64.7\%$ reduction below the \texttt{Mean baseline} of $235.9\text{ kPa}$, followed by \texttt{POD-GPR} at $86.0\text{ kPa}$ and \texttt{POD-RBF} at $90.1\text{ kPa}$) is governed almost entirely by the case-level offset $e_{\mathrm{level},c}$ ($82.9\text{ kPa}$). On the centered spatial residual $\tilde{p}_{c,i}$ (Table~\ref{tab:main} and Supplementary Table~S8), the zero-residual baseline ($\tilde{\hat{p}}_{c,i}=0$, achieved by \texttt{Mean baseline}) already attains 42-case RMS $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res,RMS}} = 2.89\text{ kPa}$ ($2886\text{ Pa}$), 42-case arithmetic-mean $\overline{\mathrm{RMSE}}_{\mathrm{res}} = 1.51\text{ kPa}$ ($1513\text{ Pa}$), and median $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 0.202$. Only four models improve upon the zero-residual baseline across all three residual summaries: within-group \texttt{Nearest case} ($\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res,RMS}} = 0.67\text{ kPa}$, $\overline{\mathrm{RMSE}}_{\mathrm{res}} = 0.30\text{ kPa}$, median $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 0.0122$), \texttt{POD-GPR} ($0.46\text{ kPa}$, $0.22\text{ kPa}$, $0.0457$), \texttt{POD-RBF} ($0.47\text{ kPa}$, $0.22\text{ kPa}$, $0.0483$), and pooled \texttt{XGBoost} ($1.01\text{ kPa}$, $0.55\text{ kPa}$, $0.151$). Linear \texttt{Ridge} achieves a slightly lower $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res,RMS}}$ ($2.56\text{ kPa}$) by partially capturing the vertical hydrostatic residual in \texttt{VHVC/large}, but degrades on arithmetic-mean $\overline{\mathrm{RMSE}}_{\mathrm{res}}$ ($1.82\text{ kPa}$) and median $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}}$ ($1.07$). By contrast, coordinate networks and neural operators inject spurious spatial fluctuations of $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res,RMS}} = 8.55\text{--}60.5\text{ kPa}$ ($\overline{\mathrm{RMSE}}_{\mathrm{res}} = 7.13\text{--}37.4\text{ kPa}$, median $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 4.53\text{--}24.3$), and multi-output tree ensembles (\texttt{Extra Trees}, \texttt{Random Forest}, \texttt{CatBoost}) yield $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res,RMS}} = 10.1\text{--}31.9\text{ kPa}$ ($\overline{\mathrm{RMSE}}_{\mathrm{res}} = 5.49\text{--}21.9\text{ kPa}$, median $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 2.91\text{--}11.1$).
  \item \textbf{Specified voxel round-trip reconstruction reference and FNO underperformance on slender pipe loops:} Evaluating the specified \texttt{Lattice floor (oracle)} round-trip operator (Table~\ref{tab:main}) shows that splatting ground-truth CFD fields onto the $64\times 64\times 16$ Cartesian grid and trilinearly interpolating back to mesh nodes---with zero learning error---already incurs median case nMAE of $0.336$ ($\rho$), $0.334$ ($T$), $0.339$ ($\varepsilon$), $0.415$ ($k$), $0.196$ ($u$), $0.241$ ($v$), and $0.323$ ($w$). Although learned spectral grid values could in principle partially compensate for trilinear interpolation blur (so \texttt{Lattice floor (oracle)} is a specified round-trip reference rather than a strict mathematical lower bound over all grid-based models), the fact that voxel widths in the large loop ($47.0 \times 63.8\text{ mm}$) are $3.4\times\text{--}4.6\times$ wider than the pipe diameter ($D_i = 13.84\text{ mm}$) causes this round-trip error alone to exceed the out-of-fold error of \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} on every velocity and turbulence field, explaining why \texttt{F-FNO} and \texttt{U-FNO3D} rank near the bottom of the neural models (Fig.~\ref{fig:heatmap}).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Exploratory statistical comparisons and paired case-level effect sizes:} Treating nonparametric Friedman tests and two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests across the 42 cases (computed on three-seed case-averaged $\mathrm{nMAE}_c$ for the seven spatially varying fields $\rho, T, \varepsilon, k, u, v, w$ and on case-level MAE in $\text{kPa}$ for $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}$, with Holm step-down adjustment across the 18 comparisons against \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} applied separately within each of the eight fields, accompanied by the median paired difference $\Delta = \mathrm{Median}_c(M_{\mathrm{comp},c} - M_{\mathrm{FiLM},c})$ and its 2,000-resample paired percentile bootstrap 95\% confidence interval seeded with \texttt{np.random.default\_rng(2026)}; Supplementary Table~S10) as exploratory, overall model differences are significant on all eight fields ($p < 0.001$). Against \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} ($\alpha = 0.05$): (i) on $\varepsilon$ and $k$, \texttt{FiLM-MLP} ranks first among pooled models (overall mean rank $4.62$), significantly outperforming all 15 pooled baselines on $\varepsilon$ ($\Delta = +0.034\text{ }[+0.021,+0.070]$ vs.\ \texttt{Concat-MLP}, $+0.073\text{ }[+0.056,+0.098]$ vs.\ \texttt{DeepONet}, $+0.124\text{ }[+0.094,+0.141]$ vs.\ \texttt{Extra Trees}) and 14 of 15 on $k$ ($\Delta = +0.150\text{ }[+0.077,+0.176]$ vs.\ \texttt{DeepONet}, $+0.186\text{ }[+0.140,+0.230]$ vs.\ \texttt{Extra Trees}; all except \texttt{Concat-MLP}, $\Delta = +0.034\text{ }[-0.005,+0.059]$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.190$), while not differing significantly from within-group \texttt{POD-GPR} ($\Delta = -0.036\text{ }[-0.069,-0.002]$ on $\varepsilon$, $-0.022\text{ }[-0.053,+0.023]$ on $k$), \texttt{POD-RBF} ($\Delta = -0.023\text{ }[-0.062,+0.013]$ on $\varepsilon$, $+0.012\text{ }[-0.036,+0.058]$ on $k$), and \texttt{Nearest case} ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \ge 0.190$); (ii) on $u$ and $v$, \texttt{FiLM-MLP} ranks first ($5.83$ and $5.26$), significantly outperforming \texttt{Concat-MLP} ($\Delta = +0.029\text{ }[+0.018,+0.055]$ on $u$, $+0.035\text{ }[+0.017,+0.066]$ on $v$), both \texttt{PINNs} ($\Delta = +0.066$ and $+0.100$), \texttt{LNO}, \texttt{Transolver-style}, \texttt{GAMS-GNN-style}, both \texttt{FNOs} (\texttt{U-FNO3D}: $\Delta = +0.153\text{ }[+0.119,+0.171]$ and $+0.184\text{ }[+0.147,+0.228]$), \texttt{Ridge}, and \texttt{Mean baseline} ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.05$, plus \texttt{DeepONet} $\Delta = +0.086$ and \texttt{Extra Trees} $\Delta = +0.103$ on $v$), while not differing significantly from the remaining tree ensembles or within-group aligned models ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \ge 0.052$); (iii) on $w$, \texttt{FiLM-MLP} is significantly outperformed by within-group \texttt{POD-RBF} ($\Delta = -0.097\text{ }[-0.136,-0.069]$), \texttt{POD-GPR} ($\Delta = -0.087\text{ }[-0.124,-0.069]$), and \texttt{Nearest case} ($\Delta = -0.076\text{ }[-0.094,-0.064]$), does not differ significantly from \texttt{PINN continuity}, tree ensembles, \texttt{Ridge}, or \texttt{Mean baseline} ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \ge 0.096$), and significantly outperforms all other neural/operator baselines ($\Delta = +0.041\text{ }[+0.024,+0.051]$ vs.\ \texttt{Concat-MLP}, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.05$); and (iv) on $\rho$, $T$, and $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}$, \texttt{FiLM-MLP} is significantly outperformed by \texttt{POD-RBF} on $T$ ($\Delta = -0.708\text{ }[-1.382,-0.048]$) and by \texttt{Extra Trees} ($\Delta = -121.4\text{ kPa }[-221.3,-35.6]$), \texttt{POD-GPR} ($\Delta = -118.6\text{ kPa}$), \texttt{POD-RBF} ($\Delta = -114.4\text{ kPa}$), and \texttt{Random Forest} ($\Delta = -110.2\text{ kPa}$) on $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}$ case MAE ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \le 0.024$), while not differing significantly from \texttt{DeepONet} ($\Delta = +0.076$ on $\rho$, $+0.151$ on $T$) or \texttt{Concat-MLP} ($\Delta = -0.060$ on $\rho$, $+0.094$ on $T$; $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \ge 0.170$).

\subsection{Limitations}
Three limitations of this study should be noted: (1) because the dataset is curated from legacy converged CFD-Post exports without original solver case/journal files or convergence histories, we cannot separate a physical flow-regime transition from convergence to an alternative steady numerical branch in the two near-stagnant cases \texttt{HHVC\_P20\_90\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_305} ($Q=20\text{ W}$) and \texttt{HHVC\_P30\_90\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_305} ($Q=30\text{ W}$), nor can we directly verify the specific $k$--$\varepsilon$ sub-variant, wall-function treatment, or inferred hydrostatic reference density $\rho_0$; (2) plane-weighted mean $\mathrm{CO}_2$ density $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ (averaged over exported sampling planes rather than volume-integrated over the pipe) varies across steady-state cases at the same $(p_{\mathrm{op}}, T_{\mathrm{in}})$ (Section~2.2), so the cases represent independent steady operating points rather than a verified constant-mass inventory trajectory; and (3) seeds \texttt{2026}, \texttt{2027}, and \texttt{2028} re-randomize the five-fold stratified case splits in addition to model initialization, and the small group sizes in \texttt{HHVC/small} ($n=5$) and \texttt{VHVC/small} ($n=4$) limit statistical power under group holdout.

\section{Conclusions}
We have presented a case-disjoint surrogate benchmark across 42 steady-state $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ natural-circulation loop CFD simulations ($5,451,341$ primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ nodes). Our main conclusions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Case-disjoint validation and two-part pressure evaluation are essential:} Across the three strictly matched-configuration tree baselines (\texttt{RF}, \texttt{ET}, \texttt{CB}), 80/20 random node splits understate five-fold case-disjoint case-macro MAE by $6.78\times\text{--}8.25\times$ on density, $11.29\times\text{--}30.08\times$ on gauge pressure, and $5.62\times\text{--}10.24\times$ on temperature ($6.78\times\text{--}10.37\times$ and $11.29\times\text{--}87.54\times$ across all four tree baselines including \texttt{XGBoost}), while \texttt{Mean} and \texttt{Ridge} controls remain near $0.95\times\text{--}1.26\times$. Separating gauge pressure into its case-level mean offset and centered spatial residual---referenced against the zero-residual baseline ($\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res,RMS}} = 2.89\text{ kPa}$, $\overline{\mathrm{RMSE}}_{\mathrm{res}} = 1.51\text{ kPa}$, median $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 0.202$)---shows that only \texttt{Nearest case}, \texttt{POD-GPR}, \texttt{POD-RBF}, and \texttt{XGBoost} improve upon predicting zero spatial residual across all three residual summaries.
  \item \textbf{Different physical fields and metrics favor distinct inductive biases:} Under three-seed five-fold case-disjoint cross-validation, \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} leads all pooled models in both case-macro MAE and median case nMAE across all three velocity components ($\mathrm{nMAE} = 0.162, 0.175, 0.272$) and both turbulence fields ($\mathrm{nMAE} = 0.192$ on $\varepsilon$, $0.242$ on $k$); on density and temperature, \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP} achieves the lowest pooled case-macro MAE ($26.3\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ and $5.14^\circ\text{C}$) whereas \texttt{DeepONet} ($\mathrm{nMAE} = 0.837$) and \texttt{PINN continuity} ($\mathrm{nMAE} = 1.350$) achieve the lowest pooled median case nMAE; and within-group mesh-aligned \texttt{POD-RBF} and \texttt{POD-GPR} achieve the lowest median case nMAE on temperature ($0.974$), $\varepsilon$ ($0.150$ for \texttt{POD-GPR}; $0.177$ for \texttt{POD-RBF}), and $k$ ($0.237$ for \texttt{POD-RBF}; $0.245$ for \texttt{POD-GPR}) when interpolating on a fixed mesh, whereas uniform voxelized spectral operators (\texttt{F-FNO}, \texttt{U-FNO}) are severely limited by coarse $64\times 64\times 16$ Cartesian voxelization (\texttt{Lattice floor (oracle)} round-trip $\mathrm{nMAE} = 0.196\text{--}0.415$) on slender pipe geometries ($D_i/L < 1.7\%$).
  \item \textbf{Cross-group generalization requires multi-scale coverage:} Leave-one-group-out tests ($128$ model--field evaluations per group) show that pooled tree and neural operators (\texttt{XGBoost}, \texttt{Extra Trees}, \texttt{DeepONet}) beat the \texttt{Mean baseline} across $\rho, T, \varepsilon, k, v$ when the loop scale and orientation are covered in training (\texttt{VHVC/small}), whereas under combined $3.4\times\text{--}4.1\times$ geometric and operating-regime shifts (\texttt{VHVC/large}, \texttt{HHHC/small}) most surrogates degrade close to or worse than the \texttt{Mean baseline}.
\end{enumerate}
