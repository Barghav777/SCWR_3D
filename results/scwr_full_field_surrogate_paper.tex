\title{\textbf{Case-disjoint benchmark of surrogate models for flow and thermal fields in supercritical $\mathrm{CO}_2$ natural-circulation loops}}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Surrogate models for supercritical carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{sCO}_2$) natural-circulation loops (NCLs) can replace day-long steady-state conjugate computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations with millisecond-scale field predictions, yet most published evaluations rely on random node-level train--test splits or single pooled metrics that mask generalization failure across unseen operating conditions. Here we present a case-disjoint surrogate benchmark built from 42 steady-state RANS simulations solved with a $k$--$\varepsilon$ family turbulence model ($k$ and $\varepsilon$ are exported) and a Span--Wagner equation of state verified against \texttt{CoolProp} (median $0.0008\%$) across three heater--cooler configurations (\texttt{HHHC}, \texttt{HHVC}, and \texttt{VHVC}) and two loop scales. Across three random seeds, we benchmark 17 surrogate models (plus an input-concatenation conditioning ablation) spanning tree ensembles, Fourier-coordinate neural networks, physics-informed neural networks (PINNs), DeepONet, Latent Neural Operator, Transolver-style attention, multi-scale graph networks, Fourier neural operators (\texttt{F-FNO}, \texttt{U-FNO}), and proper orthogonal decomposition (\texttt{POD}) reduced-order models under five-fold stratified case-disjoint cross-validation and leave-one-group-out extrapolation. We show that random node splitting understates five-fold case-disjoint error across the four nonlinear tree baselines by roughly an order of magnitude on thermodynamic state fields ($6.78\times\text{--}10.37\times$ on density, $11.29\times\text{--}87.54\times$ on gauge pressure, and $5.62\times\text{--}10.24\times$ on temperature in case-macro MAE) because high-capacity models memorize case-specific operating levels from adjacent mesh nodes. We also show that separating closed-loop gauge pressure into its case-level thermal-pressurization offset and centered spatial residual prevents small within-case hydrostatic variations from distorting model rankings. Across the eight exported CFD fields, no single architecture wins universally: among cross-configuration pooled models, \texttt{DeepONet} and \texttt{PINN continuity} lead on density and temperature, \texttt{Extra Trees} best predicts case-level gauge pressure, and multiplicative feature-modulated coordinates (\texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP}) achieve the lowest error across both turbulence fields ($k, \varepsilon$) and all three velocity components ($u, v, w$), while within-group \texttt{POD-RBF} yields the sharpest temperature and turbulence reconstructions when interpolating on a fixed loop mesh.
\end{abstract}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} supercritical $\mathrm{CO}_2$, natural-circulation loop, CFD surrogate benchmark, case-disjoint validation, neural operators, proper orthogonal decomposition

\section{Introduction}
Natural-circulation loops (NCLs) operating with supercritical fluids remove core and decay heat without mechanical pumps by balancing buoyancy forces between heated and cooled sections against frictional and local form losses~\cite{swapnalee2012generalized,vijayan2002experimental,pioro2007heat}. Supercritical carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{sCO}_2$, critical point $T_c = 304.13\text{ K} = 30.98^\circ\text{C}$, $p_c = 7.3773\text{ MPa} = 73.773\text{ bar}$) is widely used as a scaled simulant fluid for supercritical water reactor (SCWR) thermal-hydraulics and compact power cycles because its lower critical pressure and temperature permit experiments and conjugate computational fluid dynamics (CFD) studies near the pseudocritical locus~\cite{span1996new,dostal2004supercritical,bae2005mixed}. Near the pseudocritical temperature $T_{\mathrm{pc}}(p)$, small variations in enthalpy or wall heat flux induce steep nonlinear variations in density $\rho$, specific heat $c_p$, viscosity $\mu$, and thermal conductivity $k$, coupling core-to-wall buoyancy gradients with turbulence production and heat-transfer deterioration~\cite{kurganov2013heat,sarkar2014entropy,chen2013numerical}.

Because resolving conjugate heat transfer between the primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ pipe, the solid metallic piping, and the secondary cooling-water jacket requires fine near-wall meshes and real-gas property evaluations, each mesh-independent steady-state RANS simulation in our dataset requires approximately one day of compute time. Data-driven spatial surrogates---including gradient-boosted decision trees~\cite{friedman2001greedy,chen2016xgboost,prokhorenkova2018catboost}, physics-informed coordinate networks~\cite{raissi2019physics,tancik2020fourier,perez2018film}, operator-learning architectures~\cite{lu2021learning,li2021fourier,wen2022ufno,wu2024transolver}, and proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) reduced-order models~\cite{berkooz1993proper,audouze2009nonintrusive}---offer millisecond-scale inference once trained. However, three key methodological challenges limit current CFD surrogate evaluations for closed natural-circulation loops:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Node-random data leakage:} Splitting a pooled table of millions of spatial nodes randomly between training and test sets places adjacent mesh nodes from every operating condition into both splits, reducing the task to spatial interpolation within already-seen CFD solutions rather than generalization to unseen operating conditions.
  \item \textbf{Metric distortion from closed-loop thermodynamic level offsets:} Reporting a single pooled $R^2$ across all test nodes conflates between-case operating-level shifts with within-case spatial profile accuracy. Conversely, in closed loops where thermal pressurization shifts the mean gauge pressure across cases by more than $1.7\text{ MPa}$ while the within-case spatial standard deviation is only $\sim 1.5\text{ kPa}$, naive within-case normalization produces uninterpretable pressure errors.
  \item \textbf{Competing architectural inductive biases across thin-pipe loop geometries:} Closed circulation loops combine slender circular pipes ($D_i/L \sim 10^{-2}$), sharp $90^\circ$ elbows, and distinct heater--cooler orientations, making it unclear when pointwise coordinate networks, operator-learning models, voxelized spectral convolutions, or mesh-aligned POD bases should be preferred across thermodynamic state versus kinematic and turbulence fields.
\end{enumerate}

This paper addresses all three challenges through a three-seed surrogate benchmark across 42 steady-state $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ natural-circulation loop CFD simulations. Specifically, our main contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Quantification of node-split leakage versus case-disjoint generalization:} Across four nonlinear tree ensembles (\texttt{Random Forest}, \texttt{Extra Trees}, \texttt{XGBoost}, \texttt{CatBoost}) and two low-capacity controls (\texttt{Mean baseline}, \texttt{Ridge}), we quantify the error inflation between 80/20 random node splits and five-fold case-disjoint cross-validation under both case-macro MAE and median case-normalized MAE (nMAE).
  \item \textbf{Thermodynamic audit and two-part decomposition of closed-loop gauge pressure:} We evaluate the empirical pressure--temperature slope ($\approx 0.21\text{--}0.23\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$) alongside the \texttt{CoolProp} Span--Wagner isochoric derivative $(\partial p/\partial T)_\rho = 0.315\text{--}0.365\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ and plane-weighted mean density variations across cases, characterize the near-stagnant steady solutions in \texttt{HHVC} at $20\text{ W}$ and $30\text{ W}$, and introduce a pressure evaluation protocol that separates case-level mean error from centered spatial residual error.
  \item \textbf{Three-seed benchmark across 18 model variants and four structural groups:} We compare tree ensembles, Fourier-feature coordinate MLPs (isolating multiplicative FiLM modulation against additive input concatenation), physics-informed neural networks, DeepONet, Latent Neural Operator, Transolver-style attention, GAMS-GNN-style graph networks, factorized and U-Net Fourier neural operators (\texttt{F-FNO}, \texttt{U-FNO3D}), and within-group mesh-aligned methods (\texttt{POD-RBF}, \texttt{POD-GPR}, \texttt{Nearest case}) across three random seeds (\texttt{2026}, \texttt{2027}, \texttt{2028}), supplemented by four leave-one-group-out extrapolation studies.
\end{itemize}

\section{Physical Domain and $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ Natural-Circulation Loop Dataset}
\subsection{Loop Geometry, CFD Solver Configuration, and Primary Fluid Domain}
The dataset comprises 42 converged steady-state RANS conjugate heat-transfer simulations of closed rectangular $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ natural-circulation loops executed in ANSYS Fluent and exported on cross-sectional sampling planes via ANSYS CFD-Post. All 42 simulations use a $k$--$\varepsilon$ family turbulence model ($k$ and $\varepsilon$ are exported) and a Span--Wagner equation of state verified against \texttt{CoolProp}~\cite{span1996new,bell2014pure} (median relative density discrepancy $0.0008\%$) for the primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ loop fluid. Mesh-independence checks were completed prior to parametric production runs, with each steady-state simulation requiring approximately 24 hours of compute time.

The 42 cases span three heater--cooler orientation configurations---horizontal heater and horizontal cooler (\texttt{HHHC}), horizontal heater and vertical cooler (\texttt{HHVC}), and vertical heater and vertical cooler (\texttt{VHVC})---across two loop scales (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}): 29 small-loop cases ($L_x \times L_y = 0.8936 \times 1.0000\text{ m}$, inner pipe diameter $D_i = 15.00\text{ mm}$) and 13 large-loop \texttt{VHVC} cases ($L_x \times L_y = 3.0100 \times 4.0800\text{ m}$, $D_i = 13.84\text{ mm}$). Complete radial dimensions of the stainless-steel pipe wall and counterflow cooling-water jacket, axial heater/cooler coordinates, and cross-sectional sampling plane counts are provided in Supplementary Section~S1 (Table~S0a).

Each simulation is parameterized by four physical boundary/operating inputs encoded in its case identifier \texttt{\{CONFIG\}\_P\{Q\}\_\{p\}\_bar\_\{mdot\}kgs\_T\_\{Tin\}}: heater power $Q \in [10, 2500]\text{ W}$, primary-loop operating pressure $p_{\mathrm{op}} \in \{80, 85, 90, 100\}\text{ bar}$, secondary cooling-water inlet mass flow rate $\dot{m} \in [0.010, 0.150]\text{ kg s}^{-1}$, and cooling-water inlet temperature $T_{\mathrm{in}} \in \{283.5, 300, 305, 315\}\text{ K}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pairplot}, where the filename typo \texttt{HHHC\_P2000\_80\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_205} is mapped to its physical coolant inlet temperature $T_{\mathrm{in}} = 305\text{ K}$, consistent with its minimum fluid temperature of $305.87\text{ K}$). At each sampling node $(x,y,z)$, eight CFD target fields are exported: density $\rho\text{ (kg m}^{-3}\text{)}$, gauge pressure $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}\text{ (Pa)}$, temperature $T\text{ (}^\circ\text{C)}$, turbulence eddy dissipation $\varepsilon\text{ (m}^2\text{ s}^{-3}\text{)}$, turbulence kinetic energy $k\text{ (m}^2\text{ s}^{-2}\text{)}$, and Cartesian velocity components $(u,v,w)\text{ (m s}^{-1}\text{)}$. Excluding solid pipe/jacket wall nodes (\texttt{null} velocity/turbulence fields, $\rho \approx 7970\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) and outer cooling-water jacket nodes ($\rho > 950\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) and deduplicating intersecting plane coordinates isolates $5,451,341$ unique primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ pipe-core nodes ($\rho \in [153.2, 892.5]\text{ kg m}^{-3}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:distributions}).

\subsection{Gauge Pressure, Empirical Thermal-Pressurization Slope, and Fixed-Mass Test}
For thermophysical property evaluation across all 42 simulations, the local absolute pressure $p_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathbf{x})$ is related to the nominal operating pressure $p_{\mathrm{op}}$ and exported gauge pressure $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mathbf{x})$ by
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathbf{x}) = p_{\mathrm{op}} + p_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mathbf{x}).
  \label{eq:piezometric}
\end{equation}
Evaluating the Span--Wagner $\mathrm{CO}_2$ equation of state in \texttt{CoolProp}~\cite{span1996new,bell2014pure} at $(T(\mathbf{x}), p_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathbf{x}))$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:piezometric} reproduces the exported nodal density $\rho(\mathbf{x})$ across all 42 cases to a median relative error of $0.0008\%$ (case-level range $0.00004\%\text{--}0.0015\%$), confirming that $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mathbf{x})$ enters the solver's equation-of-state lookup directly through Eq.~\eqref{eq:piezometric}. Within any single simulation $c$, the spatial variation of $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}$ around the loop is small because the solver subtracts a constant reference hydrostatic head (whose reference density $\rho_0$ is not exported in the CFD-Post tables, yielding a reduced vertical gradient $\mathrm{d}p_{\mathrm{gauge}}/\mathrm{d}y \approx +0.37\text{ to }+0.94\text{ kPa m}^{-1}$ along the vertical legs): the within-case standard deviation $\sigma_c(p_{\mathrm{gauge}})$ is $0.15\text{--}0.39\text{ kPa}$ in the $1.00\text{ m}$ small loops and $1.45\text{--}8.79\text{ kPa}$ in the $4.08\text{ m}$ large loop, with a 42-case mean of $\bar{\sigma}_c = 1.51\text{ kPa}$.

By contrast, the case-mean gauge pressure $\bar{p}_c = \frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c} p_{\mathrm{gauge},c,i}$ spans $1.42\text{ kPa}$ to $1.763\text{ MPa}$ across the 42 cases ($\mathrm{SD}(\bar{p}_c) = 337.9\text{ kPa}$, or $224\times\bar{\sigma}_c$; Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Top-pinned large loop versus unpinned small loops:} In the 13 large-loop \texttt{VHVC} cases, a gauge-pressure reference point is pinned near $0\text{ Pa}$ at the top of the loop ($y = 4.08\text{ m}$), keeping $\bar{p}_c \in [1.42, 8.77]\text{ kPa}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}a). In the 29 small-loop cases (\texttt{HHHC}, \texttt{HHVC}, and small \texttt{VHVC}), the closed $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ domain has no pressure-outlet pin, so $\bar{p}_c$ rises with loop-mean temperature $\bar{T}_c$.
  \item \textbf{Empirical slope versus \texttt{CoolProp} $(\partial p/\partial T)_\rho$ and fixed-mass test:} Across the 17 small-loop cases at $p_{\mathrm{op}} = 90\text{ bar}$ and $T_{\mathrm{in}} = 305\text{ K}$ ($\bar{\rho}_c \in [672.1, 718.0]\text{ kg m}^{-3}$, $\bar{T}_c \in [305.07, 312.51]\text{ K}$), ordinary least-squares regression of $\bar{p}_c$ against $\bar{T}_c$ yields an \emph{empirical slope} of $0.214\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ across all 17 cases and $0.235\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ ($\approx 0.23\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$) across the five \texttt{HHVC} cases (Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}b). Evaluating the theoretical Span--Wagner isochoric thermal pressure coefficient $(\partial p/\partial T)_\rho$ in \texttt{CoolProp} at each case's mean state $(\bar{\rho}_c, \bar{T}_c)$ gives $0.315\text{--}0.365\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ (median $0.338\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$, mean $0.341\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$, and $\approx 0.35\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ at $\rho = 710\text{ kg m}^{-3}, T = 305\text{--}308\text{ K}$; across all 29 small-loop cases, \texttt{CoolProp} $(\partial p/\partial T)_\rho$ spans $0.133\text{--}0.417\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$, itemized in Supplementary Table~S0b). To test whether total $\mathrm{CO}_2$ mass is strictly fixed across cases within each configuration at identical $(p_{\mathrm{op}}, T_{\mathrm{in}})$, we compute the plane-weighted mean density $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c} = \frac{1}{P_c}\sum_{p=1}^{P_c}\bar{\rho}_{c,p}$ across all $P_c$ cross-sectional planes. At $p_{\mathrm{op}} = 90\text{ bar}$ and $T_{\mathrm{in}} = 305\text{ K}$, $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ is not strictly constant: it spans $671.6\text{--}704.0\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ ($\mathrm{SD} = 9.79\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) across the eight \texttt{HHHC} cases, $681.2\text{--}717.8\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ ($\mathrm{SD} = 16.66\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) across the five \texttt{HHVC} cases, and $696.8\text{--}712.1\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ ($\mathrm{SD} = 6.62\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) across the four small \texttt{VHVC} cases (while in top-pinned \texttt{VHVC/large} at $85\text{ bar}, 283.5\text{ K}$, $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ drops from $860.8$ to $161.5\text{ kg m}^{-3}$, $\mathrm{SD} = 270.83\text{ kg m}^{-3}$, as heating expands the fluid at near-constant pressure). Because $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ decreases slightly as $\bar{T}_c$ increases across the small-loop cases ($\mathrm{d}\bar{\rho}/\mathrm{d}\bar{T} < 0$), the first-order thermodynamic relation $\mathrm{d}\bar{p} \approx (\partial p/\partial T)_\rho\,\mathrm{d}\bar{T} + (\partial p/\partial \rho)_T\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\rho}$ with $(\partial p/\partial \rho)_T > 0$ explains why the observed empirical slope ($0.214\text{--}0.235\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$) is lower than the pure isochoric \texttt{CoolProp} derivative ($0.315\text{--}0.365\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$).
  \item \textbf{Near-stagnant steady solutions in \texttt{HHVC} at $20\text{ W}$ and $30\text{ W}$:} In the five \texttt{HHVC} cases at $p_{\mathrm{op}} = 90\text{ bar}$ and $T_{\mathrm{in}} = 305\text{ K}$ ($31.85^\circ\text{C}$), the non-monotonic variation of $\bar{p}_c$ with heater power $Q \in \{10, 20, 30, 40, 50\}\text{ W}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}a) arises because the $Q=20\text{ W}$ and $30\text{ W}$ runs (\texttt{HHVC\_P20\_90\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_305} and \texttt{HHVC\_P30\_90\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_305}) are near-stagnant steady solutions ($\overline{|\mathbf{u}|} = 0.9\text{--}1.1\text{ mm s}^{-1}$, $\bar{T}_c = 31.92\text{--}31.94^\circ\text{C}$, $\bar{p}_c = 6.1\text{--}9.2\text{ kPa}$), whereas the $Q=10\text{ W}$, $40\text{ W}$, and $50\text{ W}$ runs have sustained buoyant circulation ($\overline{|\mathbf{u}|} = 0.044, 0.095, 0.107\text{ m s}^{-1}$, $\bar{T}_c = 33.58^\circ\text{C}, 37.09^\circ\text{C}, 39.36^\circ\text{C}$, $\bar{p}_c = 0.361, 1.179, 1.763\text{ MPa}$) aligned with the empirical $\approx 0.23\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ pressure--temperature trend (Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}b).
\end{itemize}

\section{Benchmark Methodology, Models, and Evaluation Protocol}
\subsection{Validation Protocols and Evaluation Metrics}
Every surrogate model predicts the eight exported field variables $\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{c}) \in \mathbb{R}^8$ at query coordinates $\mathbf{x} = (x,y,z)$ given the case-level operating and geometric descriptor vector $\mathbf{c}$ (heater power $Q$, operating pressure $p_{\mathrm{op}}$, coolant inlet mass flow rate $\dot{m}$, coolant inlet temperature $T_{\mathrm{in}}$, loop extents $L_x, L_y$, pipe inner diameter $D_i$, and one-hot configuration indicators). We evaluate three splitting protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Five-fold stratified case-disjoint cross-validation (primary benchmark):} For each random seed $s \in \{2026, 2027, 2028\}$, the 42 cases are partitioned into five case-disjoint folds ($9, 9, 8, 8, 8$ cases) stratified by the four configuration/scale groups (\texttt{HHHC/small}, \texttt{HHVC/small}, \texttt{VHVC/small}, \texttt{VHVC/large}). Each seed independently re-randomizes the stratified five-fold case assignment, the four validation cases per training fold used for early stopping, the training node subsampling (up to $30,000$ random nodes per training case in the three-seed GPU benchmark and $20,000$ nodes per case in the CPU node-split versus case-split comparison), and neural weight initializations. All nodes of every held-out test case are unseen during training and standardization, and predictions are evaluated on 100\% of the held-out case nodes ($5,451,341$ out-of-fold node predictions per seed).
  \item \textbf{Within-group mesh-aligned cross-validation:} Mesh-aligned reduced-order models (\texttt{POD-RBF}, \texttt{POD-GPR}, and \texttt{Nearest case}) require a shared reference node ordering and therefore train and predict strictly within the matching configuration/scale group of each seed's five-fold split.
  \item \textbf{Leave-one-configuration/geometry-group-out extrapolation:} All cases belonging to one of the four structural groups (\texttt{HHHC/small}, $n=20$; \texttt{HHVC/small}, $n=5$; \texttt{VHVC/small}, $n=4$; or \texttt{VHVC/large}, $n=13$) are held out simultaneously while the 16 cross-configuration pooled models train on the remaining three groups ($16\text{ models} \times 8\text{ fields} = 128$ model--field evaluations per held-out group across three seeds).
\end{enumerate}

For each held-out case $c \in \{1,\dots,42\}$ with $N_c$ nodes and ground-truth values $y_{c,i}$ and predictions $\hat{y}_{c,i}$ for a given field, we compute the case-level MAE, RMSE, within-case standard deviation $\sigma_c$, and case-normalized MAE ($\mathrm{nMAE}_c$):
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MAE}_c = \frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c} |\hat{y}_{c,i} - y_{c,i}|, \qquad
  \mathrm{RMSE}_c = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c} (\hat{y}_{c,i} - y_{c,i})^2}, \qquad
  \mathrm{nMAE}_c = \frac{\mathrm{MAE}_c}{\sigma_c},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_c = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c}(y_{c,i} - \bar{y}_c)^2}, \qquad
  R^2_{\mathrm{pooled}} = 1 - \frac{\sum_{c=1}^{42}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c}(\hat{y}_{c,i} - y_{c,i})^2}{\sum_{c=1}^{42}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c}(y_{c,i} - \bar{y}_{\mathrm{all}})^2}.
\end{equation}
Across the 42 cases, we report the case-macro MAE ($\frac{1}{42}\sum_{c=1}^{42}\mathrm{MAE}_c$), case-macro RMSE ($\frac{1}{42}\sum_{c=1}^{42}\mathrm{RMSE}_c$), the median and interquartile range (IQR) of $\mathrm{nMAE}_c$ across cases, and the node-pooled $R^2_{\mathrm{pooled}}$ across all $N = \sum_c N_c = 5,451,341$ nodes.

Because $\bar{\sigma}_c(p_{\mathrm{gauge}}) = 1.51\text{ kPa}$ while case-mean gauge pressures span $1.763\text{ MPa}$, dividing total node-level pressure error by $\sigma_c$ conflates case-level thermal-pressurization offset errors with spatial gradient errors. We therefore suppress raw $\mathrm{nMAE}_c$ for $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}$ and decompose the gauge-pressure prediction into its case-mean level $\hat{\bar{p}}_c = \frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c}\hat{p}_{c,i}$ and its centered spatial residual $\tilde{p}_{c,i} = p_{c,i} - \bar{p}_c$:
\begin{equation}
  e_{\mathrm{level},c} = |\hat{\bar{p}}_c - \bar{p}_c|, \quad
  \mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c}\bigl(\tilde{\hat{p}}_{c,i} - \tilde{p}_{c,i}\bigr)^2}, \quad
  \mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c} = \frac{\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c}}{\max(\sigma_c, 1\text{ kPa})},
\end{equation}
where the $1\text{ kPa}$ denominator floor prevents division by near-zero spatial variance in the $1.00\text{ m}$ small loops ($\sigma_c = 0.15\text{--}0.39\text{ kPa}$).

\subsection{Benchmark Model Suite and Verified Hyperparameters}
Table~\ref{tab:families} summarizes the 17 benchmark models (plus the \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP} conditioning ablation and supplementary \texttt{POD-GPR}), and Table~\ref{tab:hyperparams} reports their verified architecture and training hyperparameters extracted directly from the benchmark code (\texttt{notebooks/kaggle\_clean\_case\_cv\_predictions.ipynb} and \texttt{scripts/cpu\_clean\_benchmarks.py}).

To isolate multiplicative feature modulation in coordinate neural fields, \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} and \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP} share identical network depth ($5$ residual blocks), hidden width ($256$), $B=4$ Fourier coordinate bands $\gamma(\mathbf{x}) = [\sin(2^b\pi\tilde{\mathbf{x}}), \cos(2^b\pi\tilde{\mathbf{x}})]_{b=0}^{3}$~\cite{tancik2020fourier} alongside pipe-leg centerline distance and arc-length features, and Huber loss ($\delta = 1.0$); \texttt{FiLM-MLP} modulates each hidden coordinate block via $\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)} \odot (1 + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{c})) + \boldsymbol{\beta}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{c})$~\cite{perez2018film}, whereas \texttt{Concat-MLP} concatenates the operating descriptor $\mathbf{c}$ at the input layer. Both \texttt{PINN data-only} and \texttt{PINN continuity} use a 5-layer, width-256 Fourier-coordinate MLP with GELU activations and LayerNorm trained with MSE loss, where \texttt{PINN continuity} adds a penalty of weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{cont}} = 0.05$ on the steady compressible continuity residual $\nabla\cdot(\hat{\rho}\hat{\mathbf{u}}) = \partial_x(\hat{\rho}\hat{u}) + \partial_y(\hat{\rho}\hat{v}) + \partial_z(\hat{\rho}\hat{w})$ evaluated by automatic differentiation on $1024$ collocation nodes per step. Voxelized 3D neural operators (\texttt{F-FNO} and \texttt{U-FNO3D}) voxelize each case onto a $64\times 64\times 16$ Cartesian lattice with a binary occupancy mask and decode back to query mesh coordinates via trilinear grid sampling.

Four main physical and architectural findings emerge from the three-seed benchmark:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Thermodynamic state fields ($\rho, T, p_{\mathrm{gauge}}$) versus kinematic and turbulence fields ($u, v, w, k, \varepsilon$):} Table~\ref{tab:main} highlights why both case-macro MAE and within-case nMAE are needed. On density and temperature, pooled models reduce case-macro MAE by $73.3\%\text{--}80.0\%$ ($\rho$) and $60.0\%$ ($T$) relative to the \texttt{Mean baseline} and achieve pooled $R^2$ of $0.862\text{--}0.936$ and $0.736$ by capturing the large between-case state shifts across $p_{\mathrm{op}} \in [80, 100]\text{ bar}$ and $Q \in [10, 2500]\text{ W}$. However, because the within-case standard deviation inside the small-loop cases is narrow ($\sigma_c(T) \approx 0.25\text{--}2.5^\circ\text{C}$, $\sigma_c(\rho) \approx 5\text{--}35\text{ kg m}^{-3}$), even a modest case-mean offset yields a median case nMAE of $0.837$ on $\rho$ (\texttt{DeepONet}) and $1.350$ on $T$ (\texttt{PINN continuity}), which within-group \texttt{POD-RBF} lowers to $0.974$ on $T$. Conversely, velocity and turbulence fields have near-zero case-mean offsets and strong geometry-locked spatial gradients; here \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} reduces case-macro MAE by $78.4\%$ on $u$, $80.6\%$ on $v$, $73.9\%$ on $k$, and $49.7\%$ on $\varepsilon$, achieving median case nMAE of $0.162\text{--}0.242$.
  \item \textbf{Multiplicative FiLM modulation versus input concatenation:} Comparing \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} against its matched ablation \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP} (Fig.~\ref{fig:heatmap}) shows that multiplicative FiLM conditioning reduces median case nMAE on six of the seven spatially varying fields: temperature ($1.467$ vs.\ $1.758$), TED ($0.192$ vs.\ $0.226$), TKE ($0.242$ vs.\ $0.273$), $u$ ($0.162$ vs.\ $0.224$), $v$ ($0.175$ vs.\ $0.230$), and $w$ ($0.272$ vs.\ $0.318$), whereas additive input concatenation yields lower case-macro MAE on density ($26.3$ vs.\ $31.3\text{ kg m}^{-3}$).
  \item \textbf{Gauge-pressure level versus spatial residual decomposition:} Total node-level gauge-pressure MAE ($83.2\text{ kPa}$ for \texttt{Extra Trees}, a $67.3\%$ reduction from the $254.0\text{ kPa}$ \texttt{Mean baseline}) is dominated by the case-level thermal-pressurization offset $e_{\mathrm{level},c}$ ($82.9\text{ kPa}$ for \texttt{Extra Trees}, driven by the contrast between the near-stagnant $20\text{ W}$ and $30\text{ W}$ \texttt{HHVC} cases near $8\text{ kPa}$ and the circulating $40\text{ W}$ and $50\text{ W}$ cases at $1.18\text{--}1.76\text{ MPa}$). On the centered spatial residual $\tilde{p}_{c,i} = p_{c,i} - \bar{p}_c$ (Supplementary Table~S8), mesh-aligned \texttt{Nearest case} ($\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 0.0122$), \texttt{POD-GPR} ($0.0457$), and \texttt{POD-RBF} ($0.0483$) and boosted trees (\texttt{XGBoost}, $0.151$) preserve the smooth within-loop pressure profile, whereas unconstrained coordinate networks exhibit higher residual roughness ($\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 2.91\text{--}24.3$).
  \item \textbf{Underperformance of voxelized 3D FNOs on slender pipe loops:} \texttt{F-FNO} and \texttt{U-FNO3D} rank near the bottom of the neural architectures across all fields (Fig.~\ref{fig:heatmap}). Because the pipe inner diameter ($13.84\text{--}15.0\text{ mm}$) spans less than $1.7\%$ of the $0.894\times 1.000\text{ m}$ and $3.010\times 4.080\text{ m}$ loop bounding boxes, voxelizing the loop onto a $64\times 64\times 16$ Cartesian grid places only one to two grid cells across the circular pipe cross-section, blurring radial boundary-layer gradients that pointwise coordinate networks and mesh-aligned \texttt{POD-RBF} resolve directly on the unstructured mesh.
\end{enumerate}

Because the 42 simulations come from four structured parametric sweeps rather than an i.i.d.\ random sample, we treat nonparametric Friedman repeated-measures tests and Holm-adjusted two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests across the 42 cases (using three-seed mean per-case five-fold errors) as \emph{exploratory} summaries (Supplementary Table~S10). Across all eight fields, the Friedman test indicates overall model differences ($p < 0.001$). Under paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm step-down adjustment ($\alpha = 0.05$) against \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item On turbulence fields ($\varepsilon, k$), \texttt{FiLM-MLP} achieves the lowest mean Friedman rank ($4.62$ on both $\varepsilon$ and $k$). On $\varepsilon$, it is \emph{significantly better than} all 15 pooled baselines (\texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP}, \texttt{PINN data-only}, \texttt{PINN continuity}, \texttt{DeepONet}, \texttt{Latent Neural Operator}, \texttt{Transolver-style}, \texttt{GAMS-GNN-style}, \texttt{F-FNO}, \texttt{U-FNO3D}, \texttt{Random Forest}, \texttt{Extra Trees}, \texttt{XGBoost}, \texttt{CatBoost}, \texttt{Ridge}, and \texttt{Mean baseline}; $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.05$) and \emph{not} significantly different from the three within-group mesh-aligned models (\texttt{POD-RBF}, \texttt{POD-GPR}, \texttt{Nearest case}; $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 1.00$). On $k$, it is significantly better than all pooled baselines ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.05$) except its ablation \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP} ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.190$), and not significantly different from the three within-group mesh-aligned models ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \ge 0.190$).
  \item On horizontal and vertical velocities ($u, v$), \texttt{FiLM-MLP} achieves the lowest mean Friedman rank ($5.83$ on $u$, $5.26$ on $v$). On $v$, it is \emph{significantly better than} \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP}, \texttt{PINN data-only}, \texttt{PINN continuity}, \texttt{DeepONet}, \texttt{Latent Neural Operator}, \texttt{Transolver-style}, \texttt{GAMS-GNN-style}, \texttt{F-FNO}, \texttt{U-FNO3D}, \texttt{Extra Trees}, \texttt{Ridge}, and \texttt{Mean baseline} ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.05$), and \emph{not} significantly different from \texttt{CatBoost} ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.052$), \texttt{XGBoost} ($0.157$), \texttt{Random Forest} ($0.286$), or the within-group mesh-aligned models (\texttt{POD-RBF}, \texttt{POD-GPR}, \texttt{Nearest case}; $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \ge 0.641$). On $u$, it is significantly better than \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP}, \texttt{PINN data-only}, \texttt{PINN continuity}, \texttt{Latent Neural Operator}, \texttt{GAMS-GNN-style}, \texttt{Transolver-style}, \texttt{F-FNO}, \texttt{U-FNO3D}, \texttt{Ridge}, and \texttt{Mean baseline} ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.05$), and not significantly different from \texttt{DeepONet} ($0.550$), the four tree ensembles ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \ge 0.102$), or the within-group mesh-aligned models ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 1.00$).

\subsection{Limitations}
Several limitations of the present dataset and benchmark should be noted:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Unexported solver convergence histories and steady-branch ambiguity in \texttt{HHVC}:} Because only the final converged CFD-Post planar exports are available without solver residual histories or transient continuation logs, we cannot separate a physical flow-regime bifurcation from convergence to an alternative steady numerical branch in the two near-stagnant cases \texttt{HHVC\_P20\_90\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_305} ($Q=20\text{ W}$) and \texttt{HHVC\_P30\_90\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_305} ($Q=30\text{ W}$), nor can we verify the specific $k$--$\varepsilon$ sub-variant, wall-function treatment, or unexported hydrostatic reference density $\rho_0$ beyond what is directly recoverable from the exported fields.
  \item \textbf{Non-constant plane-weighted mean density across parametric cases:} As shown in Section~2.2 and Supplementary Table~S0b, the plane-weighted mean $\mathrm{CO}_2$ density $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ varies across steady-state cases at the same $(p_{\mathrm{op}}, T_{\mathrm{in}})$ ($\mathrm{SD} = 6.62\text{--}16.66\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ in the small-loop groups), so the closed-loop cases represent independent steady solutions rather than a strictly constant-mass trajectory of a single sealed charge.
  \item \textbf{Re-randomized folds across seeds and structured case coverage:} In our three-seed evaluation, seeds \texttt{2026}, \texttt{2027}, and \texttt{2028} re-randomize the five-fold stratified case partitions in addition to model initialization, so the across-seed standard deviations reflect combined partition and optimization variance. Moreover, the small sample sizes in \texttt{HHVC/small} ($n=5$) and \texttt{VHVC/small} ($n=4$) limit statistical power in group-holdout tests.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}
We have established a case-disjoint surrogate benchmark across 42 steady-state $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ natural-circulation loop CFD simulations ($5,451,341$ primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ nodes). Our main conclusions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Random node splitting severely overstates surrogate accuracy and masks closed-loop level offsets:} Across the four nonlinear tree baselines (\texttt{Random Forest}, \texttt{Extra Trees}, \texttt{XGBoost}, \texttt{CatBoost}), 80/20 random node splits understate five-fold case-disjoint case-macro MAE by $6.78\times\text{--}10.37\times$ on density, $11.29\times\text{--}87.54\times$ on gauge pressure, and $5.62\times\text{--}10.24\times$ on temperature ($3.34\times\text{--}4.05\times$ and $2.95\times\text{--}4.39\times$ on median case nMAE for density and temperature), whereas low-capacity \texttt{Mean} and \texttt{Ridge} controls remain near $1.0\times\text{--}1.26\times$. Furthermore, because unpinned small loops exhibit large between-case thermal-pressurization offsets along an empirical slope of $0.214\text{--}0.235\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ (\texttt{CoolProp} $(\partial p/\partial T)_\rho = 0.315\text{--}0.365\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$) while within-case pressure variations average only $1.51\text{ kPa}$, decomposing gauge pressure into its case-level mean offset and centered spatial residual is essential for interpretable model evaluation.
  \item \textbf{Complementary inductive biases of operator, FiLM-modulated coordinate, and POD models:} Across three random seeds under five-fold case-disjoint cross-validation, multiplicative feature modulation (\texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP}) achieves the lowest error among pooled models on all three velocity components ($\mathrm{nMAE} = 0.162, 0.175, 0.272$) and both turbulence fields ($\mathrm{nMAE} = 0.192$ on $\varepsilon$, $0.242$ on $k$), outperforming additive input concatenation on six of seven spatially varying fields; \texttt{DeepONet} ($\mathrm{nMAE} = 0.837$) and \texttt{PINN continuity} ($\mathrm{nMAE} = 1.350$) lead among cross-configuration pooled models on density and temperature; and within a fixed loop mesh, \texttt{POD-RBF} achieves the lowest within-case nMAE on temperature ($0.974$), TED ($0.177$), and TKE ($0.237$). By contrast, uniform grid-splatted spectral operators (\texttt{F-FNO}, \texttt{U-FNO}) lose radial boundary-layer resolution due to the slender pipe aspect ratio ($D_i/L < 1.7\%$).
  \item \textbf{Interpolation versus cross-scale geometric extrapolation:} Leave-one-group-out experiments across the four structural groups ($128$ model--field evaluations per group) show that pooled coordinate and tree models transfer across heater orientations when the loop scale and operating pressure are covered in training (holding out \texttt{VHVC/small}), but degrade when simultaneously extrapolating across a $3.4\times\text{--}4.1\times$ change in loop dimensions, pipe diameter, and coolant inlet temperature (holding out \texttt{VHVC/large} or \texttt{HHHC/small}), motivating dimensionless arc-length and radial coordinate parameterizations for multi-scale loop surrogates.
\end{enumerate}
