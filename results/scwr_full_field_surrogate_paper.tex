\title{\textbf{Case-disjoint benchmark of surrogate models for flow and thermal fields in supercritical $\mathrm{CO}_2$ natural-circulation loops}}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Surrogate models for supercritical carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{sCO}_2$) natural-circulation loops (NCLs) can replace day-long steady-state conjugate computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations with millisecond-scale field predictions, yet most published evaluations rely on random node-level train--test splits or single pooled metrics that mask generalization failure across unseen operating conditions. Here we present a case-disjoint surrogate benchmark built from 42 legacy steady-state RANS simulations solved with a $k$--$\varepsilon$ family turbulence model ($k$ and $\varepsilon$ are exported) and a Span--Wagner equation of state verified for internal thermodynamic consistency against \texttt{CoolProp} (median $0.0008\%$) across three heater--cooler configurations (\texttt{HHHC}, \texttt{HHVC}, and \texttt{VHVC}) and two loop scales. Across three random seeds, we benchmark 19 surrogate model variants (16 cross-configuration pooled models, including multiplicative \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} and its matched input-concatenation ablation \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP}, plus 3 within-group mesh-aligned models: \texttt{POD-RBF}, \texttt{POD-GPR}, and \texttt{Nearest case}) alongside a non-competing voxel discretization reference (\texttt{Lattice floor (oracle)}) under five-fold stratified case-disjoint cross-validation and leave-one-group-out structural/regime shift. We show that random node splitting understates five-fold case-disjoint error across the three strictly matched-configuration tree baselines (\texttt{Random Forest}, \texttt{Extra Trees}, \texttt{CatBoost}) by $6.78\times\text{--}8.25\times$ on density, $11.29\times\text{--}30.08\times$ on gauge pressure, and $5.62\times\text{--}10.24\times$ on temperature in case-macro MAE ($6.78\times\text{--}10.37\times$ and $11.29\times\text{--}87.54\times$ when including \texttt{XGBoost}) because high-capacity models memorize case-specific operating levels from adjacent mesh nodes. We also show that separating closed-loop gauge pressure into its case-level thermal-pressurization offset and centered spatial residual---benchmarked against a zero-residual baseline---prevents small within-case hydrostatic variations from distorting model rankings. Across the eight exported CFD fields, no single architecture wins universally: among cross-configuration pooled models, \texttt{DeepONet} and \texttt{PINN continuity} lead on density and temperature, \texttt{Extra Trees} best predicts case-level gauge pressure, and multiplicative feature-modulated coordinates (\texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP}) achieve the lowest error across both turbulence fields ($k, \varepsilon$) and all three velocity components ($u, v, w$), while within-group \texttt{POD-RBF} yields the sharpest temperature and turbulence reconstructions when interpolating on a fixed loop mesh.
\end{abstract}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} supercritical $\mathrm{CO}_2$, natural-circulation loop, CFD surrogate benchmark, case-disjoint validation, neural operators, proper orthogonal decomposition

\section{Introduction}
Natural-circulation loops (NCLs) operating with supercritical fluids remove heat without mechanical pumps by balancing buoyancy forces between heated and cooled sections against frictional and local losses~\cite{swapnalee2012generalized,vijayan2002experimental,pioro2007heat}. Supercritical carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{sCO}_2$, critical point $T_c = 304.13\text{ K} = 30.98^\circ\text{C}$, $p_c = 7.3773\text{ MPa} = 73.773\text{ bar}$) is widely used as a scaled simulant fluid for supercritical water reactor (SCWR) thermal-hydraulics and compact power cycles because its lower critical point permits experiments and conjugate CFD studies near the pseudocritical locus~\cite{span1996new,dostal2004supercritical,bae2005mixed}. Near the pseudocritical temperature, steep variations in density $\rho$, specific heat $c_p$, viscosity $\mu$, and thermal conductivity $k$ strongly couple buoyancy gradients with turbulence production and heat transfer~\cite{kurganov2013heat,sarkar2014entropy,chen2013numerical}.

Because resolving conjugate heat transfer between the primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ pipe, the solid metallic wall, and the secondary cooling-water jacket requires fine near-wall meshes and real-gas property evaluations, each steady-state RANS simulation in our dataset requires roughly 24 hours of compute time. Data-driven spatial surrogates---including gradient-boosted trees~\cite{friedman2001greedy,chen2016xgboost,prokhorenkova2018catboost}, coordinate neural networks~\cite{raissi2019physics,tancik2020fourier,perez2018film}, neural operators~\cite{lu2021learning,li2021fourier,wen2022ufno,wu2024transolver}, and proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) reduced-order models~\cite{berkooz1993proper,audouze2009nonintrusive}---provide millisecond-scale predictions once trained. However, three practical issues complicate surrogate benchmarking on closed natural-circulation loops: (i) random node-level train--test splits leak case-specific operating levels across neighboring mesh nodes; (ii) pooled metrics such as $R^2$ conflate between-case operating-level shifts with within-case spatial profile accuracy, while naive within-case normalization breaks down for gauge pressure in closed loops; and (iii) slender circular pipes ($D_i/L \sim 10^{-2}$) and varying heater--cooler orientations create different inductive-bias requirements for thermodynamic state fields versus velocity and turbulence fields.

To address these issues, this paper contributes: (1) a controlled comparison of 80/20 random-node splitting against five-fold case-disjoint cross-validation across three strictly matched-configuration tree ensembles, \texttt{XGBoost}, and two linear/constant controls; (2) a physical characterization and two-part evaluation protocol for closed-loop gauge pressure referenced against a zero-residual baseline; and (3) a three-seed benchmark of 19 surrogate model variants (16 cross-configuration pooled models and 3 within-group mesh-aligned models, plus an oracle voxel discretization floor) across eight CFD fields under both five-fold case-disjoint interpolation and leave-one-group-out structural/regime shift.

\section{Physical Domain and $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ Natural-Circulation Loop Dataset}
\subsection{Loop Geometry, CFD Solver Configuration, and Primary Fluid Domain}
The dataset consists of 42 legacy converged steady-state RANS conjugate heat-transfer simulations of closed rectangular $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ natural-circulation loops solved in ANSYS Fluent and exported on cross-sectional planes via ANSYS CFD-Post. All simulations use a $k$--$\varepsilon$ family turbulence model ($k$ and $\varepsilon$ are exported as RANS closure variables rather than DNS/LES or experimental measurements) and a Span--Wagner equation of state verified for internal thermodynamic consistency against \texttt{CoolProp}~\cite{span1996new,bell2014pure} (median relative density discrepancy $0.0008\%$ between exported $(T, p_{\mathrm{abs}})$ and $\rho$) for the primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ fluid. Because the dataset is curated from archived CFD-Post plane exports, this \texttt{CoolProp} audit verifies equation-of-state consistency of the exported fields rather than mesh-independence or experimental flow validation.

The 42 cases cover three heater--cooler configurations---horizontal heater/horizontal cooler (\texttt{HHHC}), horizontal heater/vertical cooler (\texttt{HHVC}), and vertical heater/vertical cooler (\texttt{VHVC})---across two loop scales (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}): 29 small-loop cases ($L_x \times L_y = 0.8936 \times 1.0000\text{ m}$, $D_i = 15.00\text{ mm}$) and 13 large-loop \texttt{VHVC} cases ($L_x \times L_y = 3.0100 \times 4.0800\text{ m}$, $D_i = 13.84\text{ mm}$). Complete radial dimensions of the stainless-steel wall and cooling-water jacket, axial heater/cooler spans, and sampling-plane counts (97 planes in small loops; 142 planes in the large loop) are given in Supplementary Section~S1.

Each simulation is indexed by four boundary parameters encoded in its case name \texttt{\{CONFIG\}\_P\{Q\}\_\{p\}\_bar\_\{mdot\}kgs\_T\_\{Tin\}}: heater power $Q \in [10, 2500]\text{ W}$, nominal operating pressure $p_{\mathrm{op}} \in \{80, 85, 90, 100\}\text{ bar}$, cooling-water mass flow rate $\dot{m} \in [0.010, 0.150]\text{ kg s}^{-1}$, and cooling-water inlet temperature $T_{\mathrm{in}} \in \{283.5, 300, 305, 315\}\text{ K}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pairplot}). At each node $(x,y,z)$, eight CFD target fields are exported: density $\rho\text{ (kg m}^{-3}\text{)}$, gauge pressure $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}\text{ (Pa)}$, temperature $T\text{ (}^\circ\text{C)}$, turbulence eddy dissipation $\varepsilon\text{ (m}^2\text{ s}^{-3}\text{)}$, turbulence kinetic energy $k\text{ (m}^2\text{ s}^{-2}\text{)}$, and velocity components $(u,v,w)\text{ (m s}^{-1}\text{)}$. Removing solid wall nodes (\texttt{null} velocity/turbulence, $\rho \approx 7970\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) and outer cooling-water jacket nodes ($\rho > 950\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) and deduplicating intersecting plane coordinates yields $5,451,341$ unique primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ nodes ($\rho \in [153.2, 892.5]\text{ kg m}^{-3}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:distributions}).

\subsection{Behavior of Exported Gauge Pressure Across Cases}
In all 42 simulations, the local absolute pressure used to evaluate $\mathrm{CO}_2$ properties is the sum of the nominal operating pressure $p_{\mathrm{op}}$ and the exported gauge pressure $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mathbf{x})$:
\begin{equation}
  p_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathbf{x}) = p_{\mathrm{op}} + p_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mathbf{x}).
  \label{eq:piezometric}
\end{equation}
Evaluating $(T(\mathbf{x}), p_{\mathrm{abs}}(\mathbf{x}))$ in \texttt{CoolProp}~\cite{span1996new,bell2014pure} matches the exported CFD density $\rho(\mathbf{x})$ across all 42 cases to a median relative difference of $0.0008\%$ ($0.00004\%\text{--}0.0015\%$). Within any single case $c$, $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}$ varies very little around the loop because the solver subtracts a spatially varying hydrostatic reference field $\rho_0 \mathbf{g}\cdot\mathbf{x}$ (subtracting a spatially constant offset would not reduce within-case variance): the within-case standard deviation $\sigma_c(p_{\mathrm{gauge}})$ is only $0.15\text{--}0.39\text{ kPa}$ in the small loops and $1.45\text{--}8.79\text{ kPa}$ in the large loop (42-case mean $\bar{\sigma}_c = 1.51\text{ kPa}$).

Across different cases, however, the case-mean gauge pressure $\bar{p}_c$ spans $1.42\text{ kPa}$ to $1.763\text{ MPa}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Large loop vs.\ small loops:} In the 13 large-loop \texttt{VHVC} cases, the reference gauge pressure is pinned near $0\text{ Pa}$ at the top of the loop ($y = 4.08\text{ m}$), keeping $\bar{p}_c \in [1.42, 8.77]\text{ kPa}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}a). In the 29 small-loop cases (\texttt{HHHC}, \texttt{HHVC}, and small \texttt{VHVC}), the closed fluid domain has no top zero-gauge pin, so $\bar{p}_c$ rises strongly with the loop-mean temperature $\bar{T}_c$.
  \item \textbf{Empirical pressure--temperature slope vs.\ \texttt{CoolProp}:} Across the 17 small-loop cases at $p_{\mathrm{op}} = 90\text{ bar}$ and $T_{\mathrm{in}} = 305\text{ K}$, a linear fit of $\bar{p}_c$ versus $\bar{T}_c$ gives an \emph{empirical slope} of $0.214\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ ($0.235\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$, or $\approx 0.23\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$, across the five \texttt{HHVC} cases; Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}b). By comparison, the theoretical constant-density derivative $(\partial p/\partial T)_\rho$ computed in \texttt{CoolProp} at each case's mean density and temperature is $0.315\text{--}0.365\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ (median $0.338\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$, or $\approx 0.35\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ near $\rho = 710\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ and $T = 305\text{--}308\text{ K}$; $0.133\text{--}0.417\text{ MPa K}^{-1}$ across all 29 small-loop cases in Supplementary Table~S0b). Testing the fixed-mass hypothesis via the plane-weighted mean $\mathrm{CO}_2$ density $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ across cases at the same $(p_{\mathrm{op}}, T_{\mathrm{in}})$ shows that $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ is not strictly constant: at $90\text{ bar}, 305\text{ K}$ it spans $671.6\text{--}704.0\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ ($\mathrm{SD}=9.79\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) in \texttt{HHHC}, $681.2\text{--}717.8\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ ($\mathrm{SD}=16.66\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) in \texttt{HHVC}, and $696.8\text{--}712.1\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ ($\mathrm{SD}=6.62\text{ kg m}^{-3}$) in small \texttt{VHVC} (and $161.5\text{--}860.8\text{ kg m}^{-3}$, $\mathrm{SD}=270.83\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ in top-pinned \texttt{VHVC/large}). Because fluid mass-charge and solver pressure-initialization settings were not separately recorded in the legacy CFD exports and $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ decreases slightly in warmer cases ($\mathrm{d}\bar{\rho}/\mathrm{d}\bar{T} < 0$), the relation $\mathrm{d}\bar{p} \approx (\partial p/\partial T)_\rho\,\mathrm{d}\bar{T} + (\partial p/\partial \rho)_T\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\rho}$ yields an empirical slope lower than the constant-density \texttt{CoolProp} value.
  \item \textbf{Near-stagnant steady solutions in \texttt{HHVC} at $20\text{ W}$ and $30\text{ W}$:} In the five \texttt{HHVC} cases at $90\text{ bar}$ and $305\text{ K}$, the $Q = 20\text{ W}$ and $30\text{ W}$ runs (\texttt{HHVC\_P20\_90\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_305} and \texttt{HHVC\_P30\_90\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_305}) are near-stagnant steady solutions (mean speed $\overline{|\mathbf{u}|} = 0.9\text{--}1.1\text{ mm s}^{-1}$, $\bar{T}_c = 31.92\text{--}31.94^\circ\text{C}$, $\bar{p}_c = 6.1\text{--}9.2\text{ kPa}$), whereas the $10\text{ W}$, $40\text{ W}$, and $50\text{ W}$ runs have sustained buoyant circulation ($\overline{|\mathbf{u}|} = 0.044\text{--}0.107\text{ m s}^{-1}$, $\bar{T}_c = 33.58\text{--}39.36^\circ\text{C}$, $\bar{p}_c = 0.361\text{--}1.763\text{ MPa}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:pressure}b).
\end{itemize}

\section{Benchmark Methodology, Models, and Evaluation Protocol}
\subsection{Validation Protocols, Leakage Prevention, and Evaluation Metrics}
Every model predicts the eight target fields $\mathbf{y}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{c}) \in \mathbb{R}^8$ at coordinates $\mathbf{x} = (x,y,z)$ given the case descriptor $\mathbf{c} = (Q, p_{\mathrm{op}}, \dot{m}, T_{\mathrm{in}}, L_x, L_y, D_i, \text{one-hot config})$. We evaluate three validation protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Five-fold stratified case-disjoint cross-validation:} For each seed $s \in \{2026, 2027, 2028\}$, the 42 cases are partitioned into five folds ($9, 9, 8, 8, 8$ test cases) stratified by the four groups (\texttt{HHHC/small}, \texttt{HHVC/small}, \texttt{VHVC/small}, \texttt{VHVC/large}). Each seed independently re-randomizes the five-fold case partition, the four validation cases per fold, the training node subsample ($30,000$ nodes/case in GPU 3-seed CV; $20,000$ in the single-seed CPU split test), and model initialization. Held-out test cases are evaluated on 100\% of their mesh nodes ($5,451,341$ out-of-fold predictions per seed).
  \item \textbf{Within-group mesh-aligned cross-validation:} Mesh-aligned models (\texttt{POD-RBF}, \texttt{POD-GPR}, \texttt{Nearest case}) require a shared mesh ordering and therefore train and predict strictly within the matching configuration/scale group of each five-fold split.
  \item \textbf{Leave-one-group-out structural/regime shift:} All cases in one structural group (\texttt{HHHC/small}, $n=20$; \texttt{HHVC/small}, $n=5$; \texttt{VHVC/small}, $n=4$; or \texttt{VHVC/large}, $n=13$) are held out while the 16 cross-configuration pooled models train on the remaining three groups ($16\text{ models} \times 8\text{ fields} = 128$ model--field evaluations per group). Because each group pairs a distinct heater--cooler arrangement or loop scale with its own operating-parameter range (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}), this protocol measures combined structural and operating-regime shift rather than pure geometric extrapolation at fixed operating conditions.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Verified train-only preprocessing and split hygiene:} In \texttt{notebooks/kaggle\_clean\_case\_cv\_predictions.ipynb}, four programmatic safeguards prevent data leakage across splits: (i) explicit set-disjointness assertions verify that training, validation, and test case ID sets are pairwise disjoint on every fold and group holdout before any preprocessing (\texttt{results/split\_assignments.csv}); (ii) validation-case selection (\texttt{pick\_val}) draws the four validation cases per fold exclusively from strata with at least eight non-test cases (\texttt{HHHC/small} and \texttt{VHVC/large}), ensuring that the small groups (\texttt{HHVC/small}, $n=5$; \texttt{VHVC/small}, $n=4$) retain all non-test cases ($4$ and $3\text{--}4$ training cases per fold) for model fitting; (iii) input feature standardization (\texttt{cf\_mean}, \texttt{cf\_scale}) and target scaling (\texttt{TargetScaler}: $\operatorname{arcsinh}(y/\operatorname{median}(|y_{\mathrm{train}}|))$ followed by z-score standardization on heavy-tailed $\varepsilon$ and $k$, and z-score standardization on the other six fields) are fit strictly on \texttt{split["train\_ids"]}; and (iv) for within-group mesh-aligned models (\texttt{run\_group\_method}), operating-parameter standardization, snapshot mean subtraction, truncated SVD basis construction ($\le 12$ modes, $99.99\%$ energy), and leave-one-out RBF hyperparameter selection (\texttt{tune\_rbf}) are computed exclusively on the training cases within each group (\texttt{tr = [c for c in split["train\_ids"] if GROUP\_OF[c] == g]}).

For each held-out case $c$ ($N_c$ nodes, truth $y_{c,i}$, prediction $\hat{y}_{c,i}$, case mean $\bar{y}_c = N_c^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c} y_{c,i}$, and population within-case standard deviation $\sigma_c = \sqrt{N_c^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c}(y_{c,i}-\bar{y}_c)^2}$ with \texttt{ddof=0}), we compute case-level MAE, RMSE, case-normalized MAE ($\mathrm{nMAE}_c = \mathrm{MAE}_c / \sigma_c$), and node-pooled $R^2_{\mathrm{pooled}}$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MAE}_c = \frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c} |\hat{y}_{c,i} - y_{c,i}|, \quad
  \mathrm{RMSE}_c = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c} (\hat{y}_{c,i} - y_{c,i})^2}, \quad
  R^2_{\mathrm{pooled}} = 1 - \frac{\sum_{c,i}(\hat{y}_{c,i} - y_{c,i})^2}{\sum_{c,i}(y_{c,i} - \bar{y}_{\mathrm{all}})^2}.
\end{equation}
For each seed $s$, we aggregate across the 42 cases via the 42-case arithmetic mean for Macro MAE and Macro RMSE, the 42-case median and 90th percentile (P90) for $\mathrm{nMAE}_c$, and pooled $R^2_{\mathrm{pooled}}$ across all $5,451,341$ nodes, and then report the mean $\pm$ SD across the three seeds. Because $\bar{\sigma}_c(p_{\mathrm{gauge}}) = 1.51\text{ kPa}$ is small relative to between-case pressure shifts (Section~2.2), we suppress raw $\mathrm{nMAE}_c$ for $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}$ and decompose each case's pressure error into its case-mean level error $e_{\mathrm{level},c}$ and centered spatial residual $\tilde{p}_{c,i} = p_{c,i} - \bar{p}_c$ (satisfying $\mathrm{RMSE}_c^2 = (\hat{\bar{p}}_c - \bar{p}_c)^2 + \mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c}^2$ identically under \texttt{ddof=0}):
\begin{equation}
  e_{\mathrm{level},c} = |\hat{\bar{p}}_c - \bar{p}_c|, \quad
  \mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c}\bigl(\tilde{\hat{p}}_{c,i} - \tilde{p}_{c,i}\bigr)^2}, \quad
  \mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c} = \frac{\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c}}{\max(\sigma_c, 1\text{ kPa})}.
\end{equation}
For each seed, $e_{\mathrm{level},c}$ and $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c}$ are averaged across the 42 cases while $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res},c}$ is summarized by its 42-case median before averaging across the three seeds. Predicting a spatially constant pressure within each case ($\tilde{\hat{p}}_{c,i} = 0$, achieved by \texttt{Mean baseline}) defines the \textbf{zero-residual baseline}, which yields a three-seed mean $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res}}$ of $2.89\text{ kPa}$ ($2886\text{ Pa}$) and median $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}}$ of $0.202$.

\subsection{Benchmark Model Suite, Optimization Protocol, and Verified Hyperparameters}
Table~\ref{tab:models} summarizes all 19 evaluated surrogate model variants (16 cross-configuration pooled models and 3 within-group aligned models) plus the non-competing \texttt{Lattice floor (oracle)} discretization reference, together with parameter counts, per-fold training times, and verified hyperparameters from \texttt{notebooks/kaggle\_clean\_case\_cv\_predictions.ipynb} and \texttt{scripts/cpu\_clean\_benchmarks.py}. All pointwise neural networks train with case-balanced mini-batches of $8,192$ nodes per step (drawing an equal number of nodes from each training case in the fold), cosine learning-rate decay, gradient clipping at $1.0$, and early stopping (patience 10, evaluated every 200 steps on the four held-out validation cases per fold, capped at 2600 steps or $8\text{ min}$/fold on an NVIDIA P100 GPU). To isolate multiplicative feature modulation in coordinate networks, \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} ($1,069,064$ parameters) modulates each hidden block by $\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)} \odot (1 + \boldsymbol{\gamma}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{c})) + \boldsymbol{\beta}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{c})$~\cite{perez2018film}, whereas its matched ablation \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP} ($344,072$ parameters, lacking the per-block linear FiLM generators) concatenates $\mathbf{c}$ at the input layer. In \texttt{PINN continuity}, the steady mass-conservation residual is computed in physical units via automatic differentiation with respect to normalized coordinates $\tilde{x}_a \in [0,1]$ divided by each case's physical bounding-box span $\Delta x_{c,a}$: $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{cont}} = S_{\mathrm{flux}}^{-1}\sum_{a\in\{x,y,z\}} (\Delta x_{c,a})^{-1}\partial(\hat{\rho}\hat{u}_a)/\partial \tilde{x}_a$, where $S_{\mathrm{flux}} = (|\mu_\rho|+\sigma_\rho)\bar{\sigma}_u / \overline{\Delta x}$ normalizes the divergence residual before multiplying by $\lambda_{\mathrm{cont}} = 0.05$.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\subsection{Optimism of Node-Random Splits Versus Case-Disjoint Validation}
Table~\ref{tab:leakage} and Figure~\ref{fig:leakage} compare 80/20 random node splitting against five-fold case-disjoint cross-validation on the $5,451,341$ primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ nodes from the paired single-seed (\texttt{2026}) split-comparison benchmark (\texttt{results/co2\_candidate\_case\_and\_random\_node\_metrics.csv}). Whereas the two low-capacity controls (\texttt{Mean baseline} and \texttt{Ridge}) show error ratios near $1.0\times$ ($0.95\times\text{--}1.26\times$) because they cannot memorize local mesh neighborhoods, the three strictly matched-configuration tree models (\texttt{Random Forest}, \texttt{Extra Trees}, \texttt{CatBoost}, which use identical tree counts and depths in both protocols) suffer large error inflation under case-disjoint evaluation on thermodynamic state fields: $6.78\times\text{--}8.25\times$ on density, $11.29\times\text{--}30.08\times$ on gauge pressure, and $5.62\times\text{--}10.24\times$ on temperature in case-macro MAE ($3.52\times\text{--}4.05\times$ and $2.95\times\text{--}4.39\times$ in median case nMAE on $\rho$ and $T$). Including \texttt{XGBoost} (which used \texttt{max\_depth=6} on random-node splits vs.\ \texttt{max\_depth=8} on case-disjoint splits) widens the four-tree case-macro MAE inflation range to $6.78\times\text{--}10.37\times$ on $\rho$ and $11.29\times\text{--}87.54\times$ on $p_{\mathrm{gauge}}$. This inflation occurs because random node splits allow high-capacity trees to interpolate case-specific operating levels from adjacent mesh nodes of the same simulation.

Four main findings emerge from Table~\ref{tab:main} and Figure~\ref{fig:heatmap}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{State fields ($\rho, T$) vs.\ kinematic/turbulence fields ($u, v, w, k, \varepsilon$):} On density and temperature, pooled models reduce case-macro MAE by $72.8\%\text{--}79.7\%$ ($\rho$) and $63.2\%$ ($T$) and reach high pooled $R^2$ ($0.862\text{--}0.936$ and $0.736$), but narrow within-case standard deviations in the small loops ($\sigma_c(T) \approx 0.25\text{--}2.5^\circ\text{C}$) keep median case nMAE at $0.837$ (\texttt{DeepONet} on $\rho$, P90 $5.60$) and $1.350$ (\texttt{PINN continuity} on $T$, P90 $6.11$; reduced to median $0.974$ and P90 $4.33$ by within-group \texttt{POD-RBF}). Across structural groups, \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} achieves low median nMAE on the large-scale \texttt{VHVC/large} ($0.471$ on $\rho$, $0.482$ on $T$, $0.082$ on $u$, $0.088$ on $v$) and \texttt{VHVC/small} ($0.251$ on $\rho$, $0.500$ on $T$) groups, whereas \texttt{HHHC/small} ($\rho$: $1.391$, $T$: $2.458$) and \texttt{HHVC/small} ($\rho$: $2.273$, $T$: $5.363$, which contains the two near-stagnant $20\text{--}30\text{ W}$ cases) drive the upper-tail P90 errors. On velocity and turbulence fields, where case-mean offsets are near zero and spatial profiles are geometry-locked, \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} achieves median case nMAE of $0.162$ ($u$), $0.175$ ($v$), $0.192$ ($\varepsilon$), and $0.242$ ($k$).
  \item \textbf{FiLM modulation vs.\ input concatenation:} Relative to \texttt{Domain-informed Concat-MLP} ($344\text{k}$ params), multiplicative FiLM conditioning ($1.07\text{M}$ params) reduces median case nMAE on six of the seven spatially varying fields: $T$ ($1.467$ vs.\ $1.758$), $\varepsilon$ ($0.192$ vs.\ $0.226$), $k$ ($0.242$ vs.\ $0.273$), $u$ ($0.162$ vs.\ $0.224$), $v$ ($0.175$ vs.\ $0.230$), and $w$ ($0.272$ vs.\ $0.318$), whereas concatenation yields lower case-macro MAE on density ($26.3$ vs.\ $31.3\text{ kg m}^{-3}$, P90 nMAE $3.74$ vs.\ $5.66$).
  \item \textbf{Gauge-pressure level vs.\ spatial residual and the zero-residual baseline:} Total gauge-pressure MAE ($83.2\text{ kPa}$ for \texttt{Extra Trees}, a $64.7\%$ reduction below the \texttt{Mean baseline} of $235.9\text{ kPa}$) is dominated by the case-level offset $e_{\mathrm{level},c}$ ($82.9\text{ kPa}$). On the centered spatial residual $\tilde{p}_{c,i}$ (Table~\ref{tab:main} and Supplementary Table~S8), the zero-residual baseline ($\tilde{\hat{p}}_{c,i}=0$, achieved by \texttt{Mean baseline}) already attains $\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 2.89\text{ kPa}$ ($2886\text{ Pa}$) and $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 0.202$. Only mesh-aligned \texttt{Nearest case} ($\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 0.67\text{ kPa}$, $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 0.0122$), \texttt{POD-GPR} ($0.46\text{ kPa}$, $0.0457$), \texttt{POD-RBF} ($0.47\text{ kPa}$, $0.0483$), and \texttt{XGBoost} ($1.01\text{ kPa}$, $0.151$) improve upon predicting zero spatial residual, whereas coordinate networks and neural operators inject spurious spatial fluctuations ($\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 24.4\text{--}189.6\text{ kPa}$, $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 2.91\text{--}24.3$).
  \item \textbf{Oracle voxelization error explains FNO underperformance on slender pipe loops:} Evaluating \texttt{Lattice floor (oracle)} (Table~\ref{tab:main}) shows that simply projecting the ground-truth CFD fields onto the $64\times 64\times 16$ Cartesian grid and trilinearly interpolating back---with zero learning error---already incurs median case nMAE of $0.336$ ($\rho$), $0.334$ ($T$), $0.339$ ($\varepsilon$), $0.415$ ($k$), $0.196$ ($u$), $0.241$ ($v$), and $0.323$ ($w$). Because the voxel width in the large loop ($47.0 \times 63.8\text{ mm}$) is $3.4\times\text{--}4.6\times$ wider than the pipe diameter ($D_i = 13.84\text{ mm}$), this discretization floor alone exceeds the out-of-fold error of \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} on every velocity and turbulence field, explaining why \texttt{F-FNO} and \texttt{U-FNO3D} rank near the bottom of the neural models (Fig.~\ref{fig:heatmap}).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Exploratory statistical comparisons and paired case-level effect sizes:} Treating nonparametric Friedman tests and Holm-adjusted two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests across the 42 cases (computed on three-seed case-averaged five-fold nMAE, accompanied by the median paired difference $\Delta = \mathrm{Median}_c(\mathrm{nMAE}_{\mathrm{comp},c} - \mathrm{nMAE}_{\mathrm{FiLM},c})$ and its 95\% bootstrap confidence interval; Supplementary Table~S10) as exploratory, overall model differences are significant on all eight fields ($p < 0.001$). Against \texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP} ($\alpha = 0.05$): (i) on $\varepsilon$ and $k$, \texttt{FiLM-MLP} ranks first (mean rank $4.62$), significantly outperforming all 15 pooled baselines on $\varepsilon$ ($\Delta = +0.034\text{ }[+0.021,+0.071]$ vs.\ \texttt{Concat-MLP}, $+0.073\text{ }[+0.056,+0.098]$ vs.\ \texttt{DeepONet}, $+0.124\text{ }[+0.087,+0.141]$ vs.\ \texttt{Extra Trees}) and 14 of 15 on $k$ ($\Delta = +0.150\text{ }[+0.077,+0.176]$ vs.\ \texttt{DeepONet}, $+0.186\text{ }[+0.142,+0.230]$ vs.\ \texttt{Extra Trees}; all except \texttt{Concat-MLP}, $\Delta = +0.034\text{ }[-0.002,+0.057]$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.190$), while not differing significantly from within-group \texttt{POD-RBF} ($\Delta = -0.023$ on $\varepsilon$, $+0.012$ on $k$), \texttt{POD-GPR}, and \texttt{Nearest case} ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \ge 0.190$); (ii) on $u$ and $v$, \texttt{FiLM-MLP} ranks first ($5.83$ and $5.26$), significantly outperforming \texttt{Concat-MLP} ($\Delta = +0.029\text{ }[+0.017,+0.057]$ on $u$, $+0.035\text{ }[+0.021,+0.066]$ on $v$), both \texttt{PINNs} ($\Delta = +0.066$ and $+0.100$), \texttt{LNO}, \texttt{Transolver-style}, \texttt{GAMS-GNN-style}, both \texttt{FNOs} (\texttt{U-FNO3D}: $\Delta = +0.153$ and $+0.184$), \texttt{Ridge}, and \texttt{Mean baseline} ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.05$, plus \texttt{DeepONet} $\Delta = +0.086$ and \texttt{Extra Trees} $\Delta = +0.103$ on $v$), while not differing significantly from the remaining tree ensembles or within-group aligned models ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \ge 0.052$); (iii) on $w$, \texttt{FiLM-MLP} is significantly outperformed by within-group \texttt{POD-RBF} ($\Delta = -0.097\text{ }[-0.136,-0.069]$), \texttt{POD-GPR}, and \texttt{Nearest case}, does not differ significantly from \texttt{PINN continuity}, tree ensembles, \texttt{Ridge}, or \texttt{Mean baseline} ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \ge 0.096$), and significantly outperforms all other neural/operator baselines ($\Delta = +0.041\text{ }[+0.026,+0.051]$ vs.\ \texttt{Concat-MLP}, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} < 0.05$); and (iv) on $\rho$ and $T$, \texttt{FiLM-MLP} is significantly outperformed by \texttt{POD-RBF} on $T$ ($\Delta = -0.708\text{ }[-1.382,-0.048]$), does not differ significantly from \texttt{DeepONet} ($\Delta = +0.076$ on $\rho$, $+0.151$ on $T$), \texttt{Concat-MLP} ($\Delta = -0.060$ on $\rho$, $+0.094$ on $T$), \texttt{PINNs}, \texttt{Transolver-style}, \texttt{GAMS-GNN-style}, or \texttt{Extra Trees} ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} \ge 0.170$), and significantly outperforms \texttt{F-FNO}, \texttt{U-FNO3D}, \texttt{Ridge}, and \texttt{Mean baseline}.

\subsection{Limitations}
Three limitations of this study should be noted: (1) because the dataset is curated from legacy converged CFD-Post exports without original solver case/journal files or convergence histories, we cannot separate a physical flow-regime transition from convergence to an alternative steady numerical branch in the two near-stagnant cases \texttt{HHVC\_P20\_90\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_305} ($Q=20\text{ W}$) and \texttt{HHVC\_P30\_90\_bar\_0.05kgs\_T\_305} ($Q=30\text{ W}$), nor can we verify the specific $k$--$\varepsilon$ sub-variant, wall-function treatment, or unexported hydrostatic reference density $\rho_0$; (2) plane-weighted mean $\mathrm{CO}_2$ density $\bar{\rho}_{\mathrm{plane},c}$ varies across steady-state cases at the same $(p_{\mathrm{op}}, T_{\mathrm{in}})$ (Section~2.2), so the cases represent independent steady operating points rather than a strictly constant-inventory trajectory; and (3) seeds \texttt{2026}, \texttt{2027}, and \texttt{2028} re-randomize the five-fold stratified case splits in addition to model initialization, and the small group sizes in \texttt{HHVC/small} ($n=5$) and \texttt{VHVC/small} ($n=4$) limit statistical power under group holdout.

\section{Conclusions}
We have presented a case-disjoint surrogate benchmark across 42 steady-state $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ natural-circulation loop CFD simulations ($5,451,341$ primary $\mathrm{sCO}_2$ nodes). Our main conclusions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Case-disjoint validation and two-part pressure evaluation are essential:} Across the three strictly matched-configuration tree baselines (\texttt{RF}, \texttt{ET}, \texttt{CB}), 80/20 random node splits understate five-fold case-disjoint case-macro MAE by $6.78\times\text{--}8.25\times$ on density, $11.29\times\text{--}30.08\times$ on gauge pressure, and $5.62\times\text{--}10.24\times$ on temperature ($6.78\times\text{--}10.37\times$ and $11.29\times\text{--}87.54\times$ across all four tree baselines including \texttt{XGBoost}), while \texttt{Mean} and \texttt{Ridge} controls remain near $0.95\times\text{--}1.26\times$. Separating gauge pressure into its case-level mean offset and centered spatial residual---referenced against the zero-residual baseline ($\mathrm{RMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 2.89\text{ kPa}$, $\mathrm{nRMSE}_{\mathrm{res}} = 0.202$)---prevents between-case pressurization shifts from obscuring within-loop gradient accuracy.
  \item \textbf{Different physical fields favor distinct inductive biases:} Under three-seed five-fold case-disjoint cross-validation, multiplicative coordinate modulation (\texttt{Domain-informed FiLM-MLP}) leads all pooled models across all three velocity components ($\mathrm{nMAE} = 0.162, 0.175, 0.272$) and both turbulence fields ($\mathrm{nMAE} = 0.192$ on $\varepsilon$, $0.242$ on $k$); \texttt{DeepONet} ($\mathrm{nMAE} = 0.837$) and \texttt{PINN continuity} ($\mathrm{nMAE} = 1.350$) lead among pooled models on density and temperature; and within-group \texttt{POD-RBF} achieves the lowest within-case nMAE on temperature ($0.974$), $\varepsilon$ ($0.177$), and $k$ ($0.237$) when interpolating on a fixed mesh, whereas uniform voxelized spectral operators (\texttt{F-FNO}, \texttt{U-FNO}) are bottlenecked by the $64\times 64\times 16$ voxelization floor (\texttt{Lattice floor (oracle)}, $\mathrm{nMAE} = 0.196\text{--}0.415$) on slender pipe geometries ($D_i/L < 1.7\%$).
  \item \textbf{Cross-group generalization requires multi-scale coverage:} Leave-one-group-out tests ($128$ model--field evaluations per group) show that pooled tree and neural models beat the \texttt{Mean baseline} when the loop scale and orientation are covered in training (\texttt{VHVC/small}), but degrade close to or worse than the \texttt{Mean baseline} under combined $3.4\times\text{--}4.1\times$ geometric and operating-regime shifts (\texttt{VHVC/large}, \texttt{HHHC/small}).
\end{enumerate}
